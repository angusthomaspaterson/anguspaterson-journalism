Defqon.1 @ Sydney International Regatta Centre, Sydney (15/09/2012)

About the Author

gozza

view my other 9 articles

  • 0
  • 1
  • 329
Fri 21st Sep, 2012 in Event Reviews

Defqon.1 first came to our shores four years ago, bringing a festival concept that was foreign to the Aussie party-goer: a festival that was willing to go above and beyond to give its attendees an experience that they could think back on for that instant pick-me-up. So once again the International Regatta Centre played home to the Defqon.1 experience, which for 2012 was spreading the motto of True Rebel Freedom, this time ‘round to a record number of attendees.

Walking around the Regatta Center into Defqon.1 you can’t help but feel like a kid walking into a candy store – you are spoilt for choice with seven stages offering everything from dubstep to hard-trance to hardcore. Once I made my way over Defqon bridge I couldn’t help but head straight to the Red stage to see what gigantic structure they had erected and true to form it was mind-blowing, a monolithic dragon overlooking the crowd; created out of materials like traffic cones, tires and barrels. The dragon had its claws around the speakers letting people know that it would be taking them on a journey for the next eleven hours with the assistance of 10 talented DJs who were positioned at the heart of the beast under a giant Defqon symbol.

I couldn’t help but take it in and was treated to the pulsing sounds of Max Enforcer, whose set had many highlights but a definite standout was when he played On The Go which saw the crowd throwing their hands in sync with the melody, a rare sight so early in a festival. From this point on, I knew today was going to be special because if there’s anything that gets you going at a festival it’s an awesome crowd. The plan from here was to see what all the other stages had to offer because there was little I wanted to miss on the Red stage once the sun set.

Unfortunately something happened which saw those plans thrown out the window and that was Zany, as quickly as I found myself leaving for the other stages I was back where I started. The main reason I stayed was because like Charlie Sheen, Zany has only one speed: GO! His set was absolutely banging and the only people I felt sorry for were the Toneshifterz who had to follow the pounding senses that had just been unleashed.

After gathering my senses, I headed to the chill out zone which was a white dome situated at the back of the Red stage; it was filled with mattresses and chairs so you could take a breather from the madness that was Defqon.1. Once I recouped some energy I headed for the Green stage, which was a little oasis within the festival, the stage offered minimal, techno and psy-trance. The line-up was an all-Aussie affair like most of the stages across the festival – I applaud Q-dance for giving so many aspiring DJs and producers exposure instead of just filling the stages with internationals.

Next stop was the Purple stage, which was offering fresh Australian hardstyle talent; the stage was covered in gramophones and was surrounded by containers letting people know that it was the place where the fresh sounds would be coming from. Next off the list was Magenta, the stage was positioned right near the entrance and the bro-step sound was the first things you heard walking into the festival. The stage was adorned with flower’s, which was cute, and the main reason for staying was the quality of the sound set-up – a standard kept across all stages.

It wasn’t just diversity in stages that was being offered. There was everything from alcohol, massages, fruit stands, body painting to jet ski and slingshot rides which could all be purchased with Defqon.1 tokens that were easily attained throughout the day, which made the running of such a big festival that much smoother.

Once I had a couple of drinks I mustered the courage to take on the Black stage which was moved to an open area this year, which worked quite well with the overcast weather. The stage was positioned near the entrance and the stage design would have most Goths weak at the knees with an adornment of skulls, mirrors and metals. The line-up was a stellar one with the likes of Evil Activities and Neophyte, which saw the stage packed to the brim throughout the day.

The Blue stage was positioned by the lake this year and had a pirate theme, but it wasn’t the set-up that you came for; it was the tougher hardstyle, with artists like The Beholder and Digital Punk captaining the hardstyle devotees with their heavy sounds. A short walk from the Blue stage past the massive slingshot and the helipad, you could find the Orange stage that had an offering of hard-trance and tech-trance situated underneath an all seeing eye that was the stage. Josh Lang vs Nomad and Organ Donors were definite treats from the stage, and I only wish there were more hours in the day so I could have taken in their whole sets (fortunately Q-dance will be uploading most of the sets to their soundscloud), but the wonder kid Headhunterz was unleashed on the main stage, so that was my next destination.

The Red stage had been awoken by the Dragonborn, with the night sky being lit by pyros, lasers and a shit load of strobes, all you could really do was find a spot to take in the show. Headhunterz set was a rollercoaster of bass poetry and he reaffirmed why he is the #1 hardstyle DJ, putting together the perfect mix of new and old. The honor of closing this year’s edition was given to the man who provided us with this year’s anthem, Wildstylez and he did not disappoint. Opening with his bootleg of Wild Ones, he used the rest of his set to demonstrate how eclectic his sound has become and he also dropped for me the song of the day, Timeless.

Before we knew it was time for the awe inspiring end show, and like on command the crowd which was packed from end to end turned into a sea of white lights with everyone trying to take a memory home – I can try and explain it but I wouldn’t do it justice so take it in for yourselves.

There are many arguments for what the best day of the year is, around four years ago a new day came into contention and it always came around mid September a couple weeks after spring graced us with her presence and more importantly a day that marked the start of the festival season, Defqon.1. And now with the P.D.D setting in for many revelers, they will be glad to hear that they won’t have to wait another 12 months for their fix, with Q-dance announcing another festival set to launch in April, a couple of guesses for the name on the interweb have been IQON Australia and Straya Q#NT, either way we are in for a treat.
