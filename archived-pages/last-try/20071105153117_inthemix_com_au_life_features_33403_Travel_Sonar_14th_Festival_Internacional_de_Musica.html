Travel: Sonar - 14th Festival Internacional de Musica

I had been planning to attend Sonar for well over 4 years now. Friends and colleagues had gone and bragged, and each year I turned green with envy… except for this year. Regardless of what the lineup was going to be, I was there already! In true Sonar fashion, this year’s lineup did not disappoint, some would even say that it branched out and reached an even wider audience than it ever had before, with the inclusion of some “new genres” such as Dubstep and what is now becoming known as “new rave” and of course the ever increasing hip hop influence in the Sonar lineup we were once again in for a diverse treat.

Perhaps I should remind you of the headliners that performed at Sonar this year – Beastie Boys, Devo, Mogwai, Justice, Richie Hawtin, Junior Boys, Dizzie Rascal, New Young Pony Club, Digitalism, Radioslave, Rahzel and DJ JS-One, Simian Mobile Disco, Mathew Dear Band, Jeff Mills, Dave Clarke, Modeselektor and many, many more. Oh my!

On my first day arriving in Barcelona I settled in and met up with some other Australians who had made the trek. After a few sangrias too many and a great selection of traditional tapas, we set off down La Rambla in search of the location to collect our Sonar passes and timetable information.

The thing that amazed me at first instance about Sonar was the extremely well organized and professional manner in which the Sonar staff presented themselves, always friendly, relaxed and willing to answer any questions that you may have. The event may very well be on a global scale and stress levels might have been high but this did not show and their humble approach was felt with gratitude.

The true beauty of Sonar is the Sonar by day location, tucked away in between The Centre de Cultura Contemporània de Barcelona (CCCB) which is situated in the Raval district of Barcelona, and the Modern Art Museum (MACBA) – both of which are right in the thick of the city centre. The Raval district is an older style area where old world meets modern day culture. Its intertwining narrow laneways and streets are lined with cool shops and overhead apartments adorned with laundry hanging out the window to dry. The atmosphere around this area is exciting, gritty and dark in parts and light and playful in others. The absolute perfect backdrop to Sonar.

Day

On my first day at Sonar I wandered around to get my bearings and didn’t plan ahead too much, my main aim was to catch FM3, some of the accidental showcase and James Holden. At 5pm I grabbed myself a cerveza and joined the small crowd at the Hall Stage to catch FM3 play The Buddha Machine live. Founded in 1999 by Christiaan Virant and Zhang Jian, two active members of the Beijing music scene, FM3 are considered pioneers of electronic music in China. The mass-produced FM3 Buddha Machine loop box was named of the best “boxed sets” of 2005 by the New York Times and was one of the top selling electronic releases of 2005-06. Unfortunately the sound wasn’t the greatest and the Buddha machine live set was quite faint, the ambient sounds didn’t resonate like they should have, which was quite disappointing because their ambient noise can be powerful and mesmerizing (if you can actually hear it).

I found myself a nice place to sit on the faux grass area in the Sonar Village with the rest of my crew and we were just in time to catch The Invisible. The Invisible are an avant rock project that were part of the Accidental Records showcase. Mathew Herbert himself has been known to lend a hand in tweaking and customizing their sound. Their influences include black metal and Japanese classic music and pop song… something different and not what I expected from the label but very forward thinking and great nonetheless. James Holden was up next and he built up to quite a crescendo of melody and ambient sound, veering away from his usual dancefloor beats – in the first hour not even one bassline could be heard; I think the sunkissed, excited first day revelers were expecting an afternoon boogie but James held it back and showed quite a different side to his musical prowess.

Opening Night featured a Beastie Boys Instrumental live set and the other half of the double bill concert at L’Auditori was the Narod Niki presentation, this is the enigmatic project by Ricardo Villalobos, unfortunately I didn’t make it, but by all accounts it was an exciting discovery for those in attendance.

My next day was filled with the magic of SonarMatica. This was quite possibly my most favourite part of Sonar, which comes as quite a surprise to me, being the music lover that I am, I was drawn away from the sounds and into the dimly lit rooms that were SonarMatica. SonarMatica this year focused on the world of magic, presenting an exhibition based on the Third Law of the famous British writer Arthur C Clarke, which states that “any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic”. Sonarmatica was a display of installations of various illusions and tricks which proved to be so much that a few hours were spent in there.

There was major gridlock at the entrance to catch Sunn O))), so unfortunately I missed out on this one, I would have to say it was probably the most popular of all the Sonar by Day acts, next time I know to get in earlier for the popular acts in the Hall Stage. The rest of Sonar by day was pretty much spent cruising around and checking out the diversity on display, which I think this is the essence of Sonar. Go with the flow, experience new sounds and discover artists and labels that you would not have ever heard of or seen before.

Night

Sonar by night is definitely an experience in itself, after mingling in the sun all day and having a short rest in between, in no time you find yourself hopping on a Sonar bus heading for Fira Gran Via – a huge Olympic stadium complex on the outskirts of Barcelona. Sonar by night is not unlike your night festivals here in Australia, minus the sound issues;) The highlight of night 1 for me was seeing Richie Hawtin playing the most luscious of lush minimal techno, definitely leaned towards the tougher side of minimal rather than the glitchy bleeps that were heard on the second night at the Minus showcase (which might I add was aurally awesome but plagued with technical difficulties during JPLS’ set). Richie was in his element and it was so good to see a crowd of thousands heaving to Spastic. Richie took off from Modeselektor and played before Simian Mobile Disco, the programming of that area seemed a little strange to me, with Modeselektor finished off on a weird happy hardcore-esque tip and Simian going into his infamous disco digital territory.

The Ed Banger showcase on night 1 was definitely a hit with the new rave kids, fun music, and no pretension, very appropriate for an up for it party crowd. DJ Mehdi, Uffie and Justice were all fantastic and entertaining, the vibe in that area was fun, fun, fun and I’m glad I got to have a dance and soak in the atmosphere.

Night 2 at Sonar was full of memorable moments, from seeing Devo on stage in their full get up and sounding AMAZING to drooling over a leather pants clad Matthew Dear on stage in Matthew Dear’s Big Hands Band;)… and then going back down memory lane with Jeff Mills dishing out the tough techno to a stadium full cheering fans.

It was strange to see Matthew Dear fronting a band and working the crowd with rockstarlike poses and Jim Morrison-esque stage antics. I really like the new sound he is doing, albeit nothing like his production as Audion or Mathew Dear for that matter, but he has cleverly made this technopoprock sound which will appeal to a much wider audience. Rahzel wasn’t as entertaining as previous times I had seen him, but I think that is half the problem, it can be a bit boring to see him rehash the same tunes and beatboxing over and over at different performances.

My first date with Sonar was one I will never forget, I had one friend tell me that if you break the ice and go once, you are bound to want to return year after year. We’ll see what 2008 brings, but for now I’m content that I went and conquered Sonar 2007.
