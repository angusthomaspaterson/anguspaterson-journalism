If you type the name Filur into YouTube, chances are you might stumble across a few vocal house memories from the past decade. There is, however, far more to Kasper Bjorke than a bunch of commercial dance tracks he once made with his mate Tomas ‘Who Made Who’ Barfod. Add a successful DJ career and remixes for high profile names like Trentemoller, The Ting Tings and Icelandic band Múm under the nom de plume Kasper Bjorke & His Friendly Ghost to the list, and it’s clear that this is a guy who’s not content in sticking to just the one thing.

In 2007, the Danish all-rounder revealed yet another of his penchants by releasing a solo album full of cinematic synth-pop on New York’s Plant Music imprint. Having been well received by peers and critics the world over, Bjorke has followed it up with the recent release of his sophomore, Standing On Top Of Utopia – a work that encompasses those many facets that make up Kasper Bjorke. ITM threw a few questions at the Dane about his new album, his various side projects and his distaste for air travel…

Standing On Top Of Utopia sounds like the work of a producer who possesses quite an eclectic palette. Which bands or artists influenced you through your formative years? Did your folks have a lot to do with shaping your tastes?
My stepfather used to be involved in this small local jazz club, doing the bookings and such, and so I was brought up listening to a lot of jazz, but also The Beatles and Jimi Hendrix. When I was old enough to choose what was put on the stereo, I was really into hip-hop – the early stuff from the late 80s into the 90s, but then it lost its soul and innocence along with the whole ‘bling-bling’ era. That’s when I turned my ears to indie, then trip-hop and drum ‘n’ bass, and then Daft Punk came along and blew my mind with their debut album Homework. To me it’s still one of the best albums ever made. Today I listen to all kinds of music as long as it’s quality, from old folk and indie to pop, deep techno and minimal. There is music in my ears most hours every day of my life.

Some of the guest vocals include fellow Danes like Caecilie Trier, Jacob Bellens and Who Made Who’s Thömas Hoffding. Did they write all the lyrics for the tracks they feature on?
I only wrote the lyrics for Efficient Machine, where Tomas Höffding sings. Jacob Bellens actually gave me a bunch of sketches and rough demos for songs. In that sense Jacob wanted to do it in another way than what I’m used to. Usually I make an instrumental track and then pass it on to the artist who I’ve chosen to write for the song, and then he/she writes the lyrics to that instrumental. Jacob has a ton of lyrics and sketches in his drawer and I picked out two from what he sent me, which then became Young Again and Alcatraz.

Is there a strong sense of community and collaboration that exists amongst artists within the Copenhagen scene?
It’s getting a lot better than it used to be. More and more people now collaborate and form small side projects and encourage each other, but I think there is also a competitive side to Danish artists. Sometimes you can feel it; not everyone is that supportive, and some do not always wish the best for you and your career, maybe because their own might be unfolding a bit slower or not going particularly well. In that way, I sometimes wish I was living in a city with a better creative energy, like New York, where everything is “bigger” and you can really achieve without being frowned upon.

I think the video for Young Again is a good example of how you don’t need a lot of money to come up with a great music clip. It’s visually stunning…plus it has a cat in it, which is always good. How did it come about?
I’m only into doing videos with a strong concept and on a tight budget. The market for videos now is 95-percent online and these days there are so many great and creative artists that are up for making videos within a small budget. Also the access to HD cameras now is fairly easy too. The artist who did the cover design for Utopia (and my debut In Gumbo ), Florence Tetiér, is part of a creative team in Switzerland called Fortune Collective, and so she asked me if they could do a clip too. I had sent her demo versions of the whole album, as the artwork was being made specifically to fit the vibe of the music. Florence had the opportunity to pick any of the singles and she chose the one she felt best about. Now they are making a video for Heaven too, which will be out in June/July, and from the stills I’ve seen it’s going to be great as well.

As ‘Kasper Bjorke & His Friendly Ghost’ you display more of a tech-house, club-oriented sound. Are the tracks you produce under this guise the kind of thing you play when you DJ?
Very much so, yes. But then again it varies. I also play disco and weird stuff in my sets. It’s very rare that I play a 100-percent pure tech set. I like to mix things up a bit. There will be a Friendly Ghost 12” release after the summer on the Compost Black Label, which will include a Wareika remix that I’m really excited about.

Are there any plans to make more music as Filur with Tomas Barfod in the future?
Yes! We are finishing a couple of songs that will be out as singles later in the year, and we have also talked about making an album next year. Filur was the thing that got us both started in making music, and I don’t think we will ever let it go totally. Though it has changed from a full time career into a side project, but it’s perfect that way. Tomas and I will also release another project Bjørke & Barfod on Tensnake’s Mirau label after the summer, and it will include a remix by Runaway, which is great!

The word on the street is that you hate flying. Does this mean you won’t be visiting Australia anytime soon?
Indeed I do hate flying, but it’s a necessary evil in my life, so I cope with it as well as I can. I really hate flying over water. But I’ve been holidaying in Australia twice when I was younger so I’m sure I can manage another trip. Sleeping pills and red wine can help.

You look a little bit like Erlend Oye. Or maybe he looks like you. Do you get that a bit?
Haha, oh yeah quite a lot. I’ve met Erlend a few times and he told me that he gets the same from some people.

Standing On Top of Utopia is out now on HFN through Stomp.
