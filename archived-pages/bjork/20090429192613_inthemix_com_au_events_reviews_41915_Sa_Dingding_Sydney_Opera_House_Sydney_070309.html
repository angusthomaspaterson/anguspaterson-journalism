CHANGE CITY :

Sa Dingding @ Sydney Opera House, Sydney (07/03/09)

Created On March 12th, 2009 by Philippa Barr


  • Send Event To A Friend

Philippa Barr

Member Since : Sep, 2008

  • Visit Philippa Barr's profile

Sa Dingding is new China. Having released only one album in the West, Alive in 2007, in two short years she had made it from the Beijing talent shows to the Opera House Concert Hall. This is due both to her sophisticated talent and her clever way of using it. She weaves together traditional Chinese lutes and drums, multiple languages, and electronica, becoming a symbol of tradition, modernity and super hip styling.

Her audience are as gathered as the seams of her multilayered costume. A few bohemians in long skirts, artistic women with bold red lipstick and conservative Chinese families. Dingding drew a surprisingly pan-Asian crowd, I saw many Indian families and heard conversations in Japanese and Indonesian as I waited to go inside. But it was mostly overseas Chinese who wanted to see Sa Dingding. A large crowd, waiting to see a very avant-garde performer. This made me curious. Who else would this audience go to see? And did they like the show?

Certainly Dingding’s voice and movement technique are as cultured as any classical Chinese performer. She sings with a vibratory intensity that is quite distinctive and it completely overpowers the sometimes cheesy electronica in the background. For her songs she uses different languages and musical forms, from Mandarin, Sanskrit, Tibetan chanting through to the indigenous language of the Lagu people of Hunan province. She even sang to us in a language she has invented herself, in order express the peculiar emotions provoked by music. Her voice is textured, like she is singing at more than one pitch at the same time, and she is able to vary the intensity and range of sounds in random order, as if to match the different lengths of ribbon hanging from her sleeves.

Sa Dingding’s heterogenous style reflects Chinese cultural policy. Instead of a China where Han Chinese rule alone, Sa Dingding represents a China threaded through with many different cultures and languages. For her encore, Dingding calls on the translator to tell us that next track is from her forthcoming album called The Big Tree and the Small Trees. Dingding is a China who is young and old, energetic but refined, appealing to the West but keeping in real at the same time. She is attempting to master the perfect combination.
