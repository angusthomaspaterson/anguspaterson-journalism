Last night one of the most, if not the, most hyped acts of the year played one of the most similarly hyped shows of 2011. I’m talking of course about Odd Future descending upon the Sydney Opera House as one of the firebrand draws of the Modular boss programmed Vivid LIVE festival. And with all the hype that is currently surrounding this group and their maiden visit to Australia the end result of last night’s first of five local shows is sure to be discussed and debated as to whether it delivered on the inhuman levels of expectation.

Indeed, immediately after Odd Future’s first night at the Opera House wrapped up media types were huddling on the Opera House concourse to dissect what had just gone down. True to legend the previous hour and a bit inside the Opera House’s made-over Studio cave was a tumultuous, chaotic and often sloppy affair with the pack of Los Angeles kids on stage routinely flipping from provocative performance artists to blown up juvenile delinquents with mics. And yet still it’s hard to reconcile how I feel about the show.

Odd Future Wolf Gang Kill Them All

May 31st, 2011

The Studio at Sydney Opera House, Sydney

Certainly, I got what I expected with the rag-tag gang bounding up and down the Studio stage, into and onto the crowd, and up into the rafters but there was a noticeable disconnect present throughout the show. As heaving as the crowd would get and as furious as the beats often became there was a feeling that at least one party out of the audience and Odd Future was holding back just a little. Perhaps it was the weight of (very lofty) expectations placed on the LA rap terror squad that meant the show was doomed to slightly underwhelm from the start, or perhaps the they were jet-lagged and still need to work out the kinks of the Australian timezone. Or hey, perhaps I’m Danny Glover and I’m just too old for this shit – in support of this last one I can confirm that it got punishingly loud, even with ear plugs.

But that’s not to say that the show bombed or that the buzz behind OFWGKTA is about to fade. Indeed, there were clear moments of cleverness and throughout most of the set a palpable energy – chiefly from Odd Future lead foils Tyler, The Creator and Hodgy Beats – that sky-rocketed tracks like Yonkers and the late set stomper Radicals into intoxicating flare-ups of aggression. Such moments were obvious standouts from the night, but the group undercut their momentum with an unbalanced setlist which careened into each song at random and a sometimes charming, sometimes not lack of focus and polish. Obviously that’s part of the Odd Future ethos but it wouldn’t hurt to develop some better between-song mic skills so there’s not four dudes talking about over each other while another two mutter spurts of “Golf Wang” and “bap bap bap”.

What is clear from last night though is that the Odd Future hype is real and not just the product of the hype machine that’s been churning feverishly for Odd Future online. For every curious hipster standing back in the crowd nodding and bobbing to the bass thumps there was a horde of bros up the front flinging their bodies about in make-shift skank pits and repeating chants of “Free Earl!” and “Wolf Gang!” like successfully brainwashed followers of the Odd Future gospel.

The reality of it all is that the show could never have met the expectations of most. There were flashes of brilliance and spark that suggest Odd Future could have their show nailed down tight by the time they leave the country but for the first show it was probably best experienced by those with the ability to switch off their lights for an hour and get fucked up with the masses in the middle of a mosh circle. Swagger out.
