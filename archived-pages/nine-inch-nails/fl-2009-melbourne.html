    Nine Inch Nails, Jaguar Love @ Festival

    The night kicked off with support act Jaguar Love, a group compiled of ex-members of The Blood Brothers and Pretty Girls Make Graves. The Portland band warmed up for their Soundwave slot with an impressive performance, and warmed up the crowd for the main-event. While they didn’t appear to grab everybody’s attention (an admittedly hard job when you are supporting one of the biggest alternative bands in the world), singer Johnny Whitney did arouse questions of ‘is that a dude or a chick?’ with his occasionally shrill voice. While a lot of people did use their slot to get as many drinks in as possible, those who did stand around to watch were treated to a good supporting performance.

    This was an historic night for Trent Reznor and his revolving band of merry musicians. After all, Reznor touted the demise of their touring future in a recent interview, and even he appeared whistfully nostalgic throughout their set. Considering the last time this punter witnessed the full-frontal assault of a Nine Inch Nails show at the former Metro last year, Trent dragged the band offstage before their encore and refused to finish the set, the result of some rather passive heckling. However Reznor was in fine spirits tonight, conversing with the crowd and generally being the kind of charismatic front-man that he can be.
    Throughout the set he paused between songs to thank the loving crowd for their support, and explained that since he had announced the possible end, all their shows from there on had taken on a greater significance and had become a whole lot more important to him.

    Right from the very first song, it was obvious to all that the Nine Inch Nails had turned up to rock the house, treat their devotees to a special night and possible final show, and wheel out many of the songs that made them the premier industrial powerhouse they are today. Songs like Reptile, Dead Souls (the Joy Division cover found on the first Crow soundtrack), and The Wretched were unexpectedly played, much to the delight of the audience. It must be said however that, while staples such as March of the Pigs and Head Like a Hole were enthusiastically received, songs from the last two albums failed to induce the same response, even though they do translate exceptionally well live.

    Not long before this tour, Keyboardist Alessandro Cortini announced he would be leaving the band, and drummer Josh Freese also announced that he would not be continuing on with his extensive musical duties to spend more time with his family. It goes without saying that this would inevitably impact on their live performance. To counteract this the band seamlessly swapped their instruments regularly, with Reznor taking most of the keyboard duties, however new drummer, Ilan Rubin (Lostprophets), occasionally relented on his intense pounding on the skins to take up the keyboard mantle and allow Trent to focus more on his vocals. It must be said here too that Rubin, who bears an uncanny resemblance to Animal from the Muppets when he’s playing, turned out possibly the most impressive performance for the evening, and easily covered for the always reliable Freese. But far from impacting negatively on the band’s set, this unusual arrangement actually appeared to enhance the sound of most songs. The lack of bass and keys throughout some songs actually worked in their favour, creating a more stripped back and raw sound, and made the floor of Fetival Hall literally pulsate.

    While it seams strange to say after witnessing this gig, it is appropriate to say that Reznor has made the right call on the future of the band, after all nobody wants to deteriorate into mediocrity. The last three Nine Inch Nails albums were not well received by their hard-core fans, and it showed when these songs were showcased live. So while it would be a shame to never again see this great band on our shores again, this show was one hell of a way to bow out.
