Jaytech: I get Anjunadeep

Angus Paterson · 27 April 2011 · inthemix

It wasn’t that long ago that James Cayzer, aka Jaytech, was one of Australia’s most talented, yet undiscovered production prodigies, hidden away in relative obscurity in his beloved hometown of Canberra, with his talents in crafting beautiful melodic house appreciated by only a select few. Eventually though, one such “appreciator” that he ended up recruiting was UK trance trio Above & Beyond, who snapped him up to produce for their Anjunadeep label, the underground house younger sibling of their wildly successful Anjunabeats trance label.

Several years down the track, and both Jaytech and the Anjunadeep label are loved far and wide by fans around the world, to the point where the stable is very nearly giving Anjunabeats a run for its money. Jaytech is back in the spotlight now with the release of the already critically acclaimed Anjunadeep:03, the annual collection of the label’s latest and greatest that he’s mixed alongside label A&R James Grant. Here Jaytech speaks to ITM about the new compilation from his adopted home of Berlin.

You’ve said you’ll remain in Berlin for at least the next six to twelve months. Is there any difficulty in being a little detached from the rest of the Anjunabeats crew over in London?
London not so much because it’s just an hour’s flight either way, and it’s dirt cheap to go over there whenever I want, and I have the right Visa sorted now with the UK where I can come and go as I please. So London is actually pretty good in that respect.

But Australia is certainly a little bit different because it’s on the other side of the world. I only get to come back maybe two or three times a year. But my reasoning for moving to Berlin in the first place was so that I could be based in Europe, and still be close to the UK, so that’s worked out pretty well so far.

Returning to visit Australia two or three times a year is still a reasonable amount, I’d say.
I can’t really complain about that. It’s enough for me coming back several times a year to stop me from becoming really homesick, because I know the next time that I see my family and friends is just around the corner. But yeah, usually I get out of here around November every year and spend the last two months or so of the year back in Australia, just because the weather is so much nicer around that time of the year, and it’s nice to come home.

So the new compilation was finally released last month. I’ve definitely been enjoying it, and the response anecdotally seems pretty good so far. What sort of feedback have you been getting?
I’ve spent the last few weeks over in Miami promoting it there, and the US crowd has been lapping it up, there’s been lots of happy fans, so that’s been great. We got a four star review from DJ Mag, and in the very first day of release we got to the #1 spot in the US iTunes dance albums chart. The result of those two things combined means it’s been getting a lot of press and feedback. It’s been a lot of work, but it’s all starting to pay off now which is great.

There’s a lot of good tracks over two CDs, and it’s almost really surprising that the label has that much output that you can sustain a release that big. What sort of work goes into putting together the compilation?
The first thing we had to do was to get in touch with everyone who had contributed to the past two compilations, or had contributed core singles to Anjunadeep in the past. We’re up to 100 releases just with the Anjunadeep side of the label now, and we’ve got so many people on the team to write tracks in that style and submit them to us. So it’s actually more a matter of narrowing down the hundreds of tracks that we got sent into the 28 or so that we actually wanted to use. So there’s definitely no lack of good music out there floating around out there, it’s just a matter of working out where it is and who’s making it, and bringing it into a compilation that makes sense and carries the identity that the label has built up over the past few years.

Do you feel like it’s getting to the stage where the Anjunadeep label is on par with Anjunabeats in terms of its popularity, and how much demand there is for that music?
I think that’s really hard to say, because the direction of the label in the first place was to do the deeper, more underground side of this kind of music. But with the following it has been gathering over the past couple of years, it’s been taking the main stage and while it’s still very much the baby brother of Anjunabeats, I think it’s really starting to stand up by itself. Whether it sees the same success that Anjunabeats has is just a matter of time I suppose.

If you look at last year’s compilation, James Grant’s disc possibly had the edge in terms of how the tracks fit together to create a bigger picture. But this year your disc takes the prize in terms of how the tracks come together to create the mix.
I think James’ CD this time around is probably more representative of what the Anjunadeep sound is all about. The one remark I’ve been getting about my CD a lot is that more so than necessarily sounding like an Anjunadeep CD, it sounds like a Jaytech CD. Which I’m more or less happy with, as well.

If you were to break down the tracks from the last compilation, as well as this one, it doesn’t really mean much to say that one CD trumps the other, because basically we just have a big pool of tracks and then we divvy them up according to each of our styles. So it just comes down to a matter of which style receives the best tracks for a particular compilation.

What I liked about this year’s compilation is that, like last year’s release, the first CD is quite deep while the second has a little bit more euphoria to it. And even more so this year with your mix, it seems you’re happy to embrace the really upbeat, euphoric side of things.
Absolutely. My sound has definitely evolved a little bit over the past few years, because of the events that I’ve been playing at and the crowd responses that I get from people when I play out. My personal goal is to try and retain the kind of melodic sound that I’ve always been known for, as well as refine it as I go along, so I can make people give it up on the dancefloor, basically.

That’s always been goal #1, and just learning how to do that, what makes people go crazy, what works and what doesn’t. It’s a very exciting process, and it’s getting better all the time. One thing that I’ve really noticed is that you can still be very musically complex, but the underlying rhythm and groove of your track, if you can make that as simple as possible in clever ways, that seems to go down the best. It’s always a fine line trying to find that happy medium between dancefloor sensibility and musicality.

Really complex arrangements don’t always necessarily translate that well in the club.
That’s right. That doesn’t mean that the club should be free of musically complex arrangements, so once again it’s finding that balance. It’s the same way I feel about techno music, and tech house, I think that style of music is very useful in your DJ set, and it can really be used to your advantage. But I also believe that it’s something that should be mixed in with a few other styles, rather than two hours of the same thing basically.

Do you find yourself drawn more and more to the really upbeat, ‘big’ sounds that you find on this compilation?
It depends on the event basically. If I’m headlining the night basically, I’ll usually start with much deeper sounds. My usual sound is a lot more house-orientated. But if I’m warming up for a trance DJ, then I’ll tend to make things a lot more banging towards the end of my set. Deeper house sounds are hardly what people want to hear on the night. But also, it’s about using these events to promote the progressive house side as well, so still the #1 style of music that I’m going to play when I get up there is melodic and progressive house.

A few years down the track after you decided to leave Canberra, and you formed that relationship with Above and Beyond. Are you surprised with how much success you’ve achieved, and how much you’ve managed to make it work for you?
I’ve definitely done everything that I set out to do in the first place, it was always my dream to gravitate over to the UK and get immersed in the scene over there. I think my heart will always be in Canberra, and I’ll always come back, it’s not just something that I can do away with. As much crazy stuff has been happening over the past few years and as many cool places that I’ve been, I think Canberra is my favourite place in the world. So it’s been really build on the exposure that I’ve had, and I’m looking forward to doing it bigger and better in the future, but there’s always going to be a little piece of me that’s always going to be a Canberra boy.

Anjunadeep:03 is out now on onelove Recordings. Read the inthemix review.
