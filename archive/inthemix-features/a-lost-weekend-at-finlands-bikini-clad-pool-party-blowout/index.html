A lost weekend at Finland’s bikini-clad pool party blowout

Angus Paterson · 30 April 2014 · inthemix

In the depths of winter in Helsinki, the temperature plunges well below zero and the sun shines for less than two hours a day. So it’s not the first place you’d think of to host a pool party. But perhaps that’s why Finland’s Waterland festival is spoken about in such hushed and reverent tones, with its combination of icy weather, indoor pool partying, solid club tunes and hundreds of statuesque Finnish women wearing bikinis.

What makes Waterland possible is the Serena waterpark, a sprawling artificial vista heated to an almost tropical temperature and capable of housing thousands of swimsuit-clad bodies. Its fake rock formations give the convincing impression of a waterfall-filled jungle, while waterslides with evocative names like the ‘Tornado’ and the ‘Black Hole’ snake up and down all over the place and scattered ceiling columns take on a grand, coliseum vibe when the lights are low, especially when the impressive amount of lighting and production is brought in for Waterpark on the first Friday and Saturday of each March.

The Friday night of Waterland is the more subdued of the two, although there’s still a huge crowd gathered early and clustered on the massive walking bridge that crosses the expanse of the pool, leading from the dance floor to the slides and bar areas. In front of the DJ booth is the shallow main pool, and swimming backwards will find you on a large, sprawling podium-style platform; it’s like the space was made for a dance party.

But the main attraction at Waterland is the crowd itself: while the Finnish might be cruelly deprived of the chance to hit the beach for much of the year, at Waterland you wouldn’t ever know it; there are enough skimpy swimsuits to clothe a small island nation. After months of being cooped up inside the energy is flirtatious but also, thanks to the down-to-earth streak that’s at the core of Finnish culture, pretension-free.

Finland’s own Heavyweight DJs kick things off on Friday night. A four-person strong collective, they bring a polished showing of mainstage bombast with an assortment of high-impact tunes largely from their own studio, focusing on big hooks and heavy drops, before handing the reins to headliner Mark Knight of Toolroom Records. Knight brings a near-perfect vibe with a cleverly balanced mix of slamming tech house grooves and pummeling buildups (that get the crowd splashing high enough to nearly soak the DJ booth), as well as a procession of anthems and bootlegs that keep the energy high. His remixes of Man With the Red Face and You’ve Got the Love all get a whirl in his crowd-pleasing closing set.

When Saturday night rolls around, the air of exhilaration is reaching its peak, with hundreds of bodies being swept through the artificial rapids and flinging themselves down the massive slides. Swimsuit-clad punters are crowding onto an outdoor terrace for a cigarette, draped in the white IKEA blankets provided at the door to complement the sparsely spread heaters. Luckily for the smokers, the temperature is relatively forgiving this year at a touch below zero degrees. Last year, it would have been around minus 35.

A group of girls are talking about life in Helsinki and how little they think there is to do in the city. “I’ve lived here in Helsinki for nearly 20 years,” says one, “But I have to say that Finland is boring. SO boring!” It’s a Finnish mantra that there’s nothing fun to do in their hometown. ‘It’s cold, dark, boring… Why on earth would anyone want to travel there?’ For overseas visitors though, especially dance music fans, the attractions are myriad, and not limited to Waterland’s obvious assets.

Boring or not, there’s good vibes floating in the Finnish air tonight. The crowd’s a little older than Friday night, with a house and techno vibe to match, and local duo Something Good are in control early in the evening, bringing a driving collection of disco and tribal house. Later, Helsinki veterans Heikki L and Orion partner for a back-to-back set, dropping pumping tech house grooves that pitch gradually towards more melodic mainroom vibes, before Italy’s Federico Scavo finishes things off with a tight mix of tech house and classics.

The side-room, meanwhile, is hosted in a dark, artificial rock cave and devoted entirely to trance, with Finnish veteran Orkidea hosting alongside UK mainstay Solarstone, both representing their ‘pure trance’ sound, while Anjunabeats duo Super8 & Tab delve into the groovier end of the trance spectrum, and Proteus brings the hard dance stomp on the final leg.

The party’s end just before dawn seems abrupt, as all the slides are switched off and the revelers are swept out of the artificial fantasy land that is the Serena and into the reality of subzero temperatures. The weather is relatively forgiving for now, but within a week the whole city will be several feet deep in snow. But never fear: Waterland will be back in 2015.
