Jona: Getting Physical in Australia

Angus Paterson · 26 July 2007 · inthemix

One of the youngest signings to uber-cool German label Get Physical, Belgian producer Jona will be making his debut visit to Australia in late July and early August, playing intimate club shows along the east coast. Although he’s made a name for himself in the electronic music scene, Jona was originally a guitar player, finding himself more influenced by Joe Satriani over Laurent Garnier. However this was all to change, although his background in blues has shaped his deep and studied sound. Having trained as a sound engineer he now finds himself at home on the Get Physical label (Booka Shade, MANDY), and is currently recording his debut LP for the label.

For the uninitiated, how would you describe your sound?

It is not easy to define my music… maybe I am not a good person to do that, but I would say; neo-house allying groove and musicality. A mixture of all different influences I’ve had in my life, going from rock to blues, jazz, techno, house… I do what I feel and I hope people will get into it. That is the way my music is, from my heart, soul, flesh and bones.

You’ve done quite a few releases on the Get Physical label, which should have done a lot for your exposure as it’s so highly regarded within the tech and minimal genre. How much has this association helped get your name out there?

It sure helped. I’ve been approached by other labels thanks to the first GPM releases. I am really thankful for that. It was an honour for me to be invited as a part of the family, and I feel very comfortable with them.

How do you feel to be releasing music on the same label as the likes of Booka Shade and M.A.N.D.Y., who are so loved and respected by their fans?

It’s good, but that’s not the most important. I dont feel it different than releasing on Fumakilla, Toys For Boys or Resopal… I don’t see MANDY or Booka Shade as big stars, even if they surely are. I see them as members of the same musical family, a bunch of very talented people and friends.

What is it about your sound that sets you apart from the other producers out there at the moment, what energy do you try to bring to your music?

The energy is coming from my background. Which, as I said before, comes from techno, blues and rock. I don’t think I bring any kind of energy on purpose, I let the flow come. When I produce a track, or just mess with my equipment, it is a very intense and personal moment.

Something that comes across in your tunes is the bringing together of warm melodies with the deeper side of club music. Was this what you were aiming for?

Yes. I think “deep” is a good term to use. I like to put melody in my tunes, not only beats. I need feelings and emotions, but of course I also want people to dance. So I try to combine melodic lines, basses, deep sounds. I pay attention to the harmony, but always with a strong sound and strong beats.

What sort of flow do you bring to a club with your DJ sets? Is it very much in the vein of the minimal tech sound of the moment, or do you like to mix it up?
I like to play really rhythmic tracks, with percussions and a more tech house feeling, as well as some old techno ones that I’ve always loved. I want to take the audience on a journey, make them have a very unique moment that I hope they’ll remember. I am there for the people on the dancefloor, I am not the kind of DJ who only plays for himself. I like to share emotions and my energy through the music I play.

You’ve got a background as a blues musician, as well as training as a sound engineer. How has this helped you as an artist in creating dance music? Has it given you a stronger understanding of musicality that you then apply to your production?
Of course. That’s exactly what I am doing, I take all the knowledge in theory I learnt in those years and apply them to electronic music. It helps to write easier, but I don’t have to be ruled by theory. Sometimes techno and house music needs to be a little off key, or sound a little weird… so I have to find the right balance between my musical skills and my feelings. The sound engineer lessons really help, I don’t waste too much time trying to find out which way to compress a track, for example. It helps me get straight to the point.

And what was it that drew you to dance music in the first place?
Listening to DJs playing techno when I first went to raves, around 15 years old. When you are that age you want to discover things. I started going out with my friends… You can’t really escape electronic music in Belgium, we have a huge background in it, so I got into it too.

What are you expecting on your upcoming tour of Australia?
To discover new things, new people, new landscapes… and to see what’s happening there musically. It will be a new experience for me and I look forward to it. I am sure it will be great.

Don’t miss Jona on his debut Australian tour:

Fri 3 Aug – Elsewhere Bar, Gold Coast
Sat 4 Aug – F4, Melbourne
