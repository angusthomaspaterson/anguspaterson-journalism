Party like the locals: Inside Berlin's hidden hotspots

Angus Paterson · 15 November 2013 · inthemix

This article is part of a new series of Music Tour City Guides put together with our partner Miller – for more inside info on clubbing in Berlin, visit the Miller Music Tour’s Berlin Hub.

In a city as creative and eclectic as Berlin, nightlife is always going to be about much more than the household names like Berghain and Watergate. And no city in the world does ‘club in a reclaimed space’ better than the German capital – from dancefloors in abandoned swimming pools to rooftop gardens, Berlin is a veritable utopia of ‘off the beaten track’ party spots. Here are five of the best.

://about blank

Address: Markgrafendamm 24c, Friedrichshain, 10245 Berlin

Website.

Friedrichshain’s ://about blank is the perfect example of how even the unassuming clubs in Berlin still boast a setup that’d be the envy of any other club in the world. Hidden on a desolate looking street in East Berlin, with a gritty underground vibe, it’s ://about blank’s down-to-earth atmosphere and crowd that makes it a winner. And in a city as famously chilled out as Berlin, that’s saying something.

://about blank is a little more rough around the edges than some of Berlin’s other notable venues, though that’s what gives it its charm. “Black” is the overriding colour scheme, and there’s a long, wide-open main-room that lends itself well to the easygoing atmosphere, as well as a smaller boxed-in backroom that’s suitably claustrophobic, flanked by a wall of couches if it all gets a little too much. ://about blank’s hidden jewel, though, is its massive courtyard area, perfect for hosting open air events in the summer months. Music wise, the club is appropriately gutsy with a winning selection of techno and experimental music that goes beyond the usual suspects, plus it’s one of the only venues in Berlin that regularly plays host to underground dubstep.

Salon Zur Wilden Renate

Address: Alt Stralau 70 Friedrichshain, 10245 Berlin

Website

One of the city’s wonderfully quirky and utterly unique clubs, Renate is housed in a dilapidated multi-floor apartment building in Friedrichshain that’s been refurbished for the occasion – though really it’s like the original living residence has barely been overhauled at all; that’s the intentional source of the club’s charm. There are multiple rooms and dancefloors across the different levels, with a maze-like layout that lends to the vibe that you’re actually walking through somebody’s house – or one that’s been sucked through to some colourful, off-kilter dimension. The ‘room’ chosen as the main dancefloor might change from week to week, along with the cast of colourful decorations – lampshades, chandeliers, lava lamps – meaning the experience seems a little different every time you visit.

There’s a large outdoor courtyard, which is also open as a bar during the summer, including a long wooden cabin with another bar, DJ booth and dancefloor, as well as several comfortable tree swings. Renate’s other main attraction is open earlier in the evenings before the actual club opens its doors – the mysterious indoor labyrinth built across the different levels of the old apartment building, that’s so mind-blowingly weird it makes a Lewis Carroll adventure look positively pedestrian. With all these bizarre elements taken together, Salon Zur Wilden Renate is a triumph of imagination, and a treat for those looking for an adventure beyond your standard clubbing experience.

Golden Gate

Address: Mainzer Straße 13 Mitte, 12053 Berlin

Website.

Famed as one of Berlin’s grittiest, most hedonistic spots with a vibe that’s always just a little wild and unleashed, Golden Gate can be found underneath the shaky Jannowitzbrücke rail line, just a short walk from the Alexanderplatz Tower; as soon as you descend those wooden stairs you’ll feel as far away from civilisation as you could get. It boasts a modestly sized, no-frills setup because after all, sometimes a rough and dirty atmosphere is exactly what you’re after.

There’s a single, modest dancefloor downstairs that shares the sort of retro decor you’d see at one of Berlin’s countless comfortable and low-key bars, as well as an equally modest outdoor garden area for when you need to take a breather, and a spiral staircase that leads up to a set of bathrooms that look about as desolate and industrial as East Berlin after the Wall came down. The music policy is suitably low-key, with a selection of fresh faces from the city’s tech house scene, and during summer the parties typically run from Thursday night until Monday morning.

Club Humboldthain

Address Hochstraße 46 Wedding, 13357 Berlin

Website

One of the recent additions to Berlin’s vast portfolio of clubs, Club Humboldthain opened its doors just a few months ago and is our pick for the underdog venue that could well prove to be the city’s best new spot come next year’s summer. It’s in the rough-around-the-edges district of Wedding, and while the area isn’t yet known for its thriving nightlife, the cheap real estate is drawing in all the students, artists and musicians and, as such, it’s next on the hit-list to be transformed into a bustling, ‘ubercoolische’ neighbourhood.

Club Humboldthain is right next to the S-bahn station of the same name, and just around the corner from the popular Stattbad club; and it’s yet another unassuming Berlin venue that’s actually extravagantly decked out with a sandy outdoor area that’s the stuff summertime dreams are made of. Add to that a very functional indoor area with room for several dancefloors and you’ve got a Berlin hotspot in the making. With the right bookings and the right promoters to steer the club’s musical direction, it’ll make for the perfect space for showcasing underground music, and will definitely be worth keeping an eye on in 2014.

Loftus Hall

Address: Maybachufer 48 Neukölln, 12045 Berlin

Website

One of Berlin’s delightfully low-key options, Loftus Hall is a great choice if you’re out to make some house and techno discoveries, instead of going for the tried-and-tested DJ names you’ll find elsewhere. It’s home to an impressive selection of showcase nights for boutique electronic labels, and it’s a great place to discover new sounds.

Loftus Hall is in Neukölln, one of Berlin’s most thriving districts at the moment, but its unassuming location means you won’t find it unless you go looking for it, making it ideal for those who want to avoid the typical tourist traps. Inside, the décor is just as relaxed and unassuming; it looks like it’s been untouched since it opened 50 years ago, with the granny curtains, old wood panelling and maroon carpeting giving it the genuine feel of an original Berliner ‘Kneipe’ (pub). Revel in those house party vibes.
