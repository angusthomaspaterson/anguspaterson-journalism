Ashley Mar: Shooting people for fun

Angus Paterson · 21 June 2007 · inthemix

Otherwise know as spinal_faun – even if you don’t recognise the name, if you go clubbing even occasionally in Sydney then it’s likely you seen ITM contributor Ashley Mar around the traps and probably had your photograph taken by him. He began contributing to the site in 2004 and has been a constant fixture in our city’s clubs since then, often popping round to as many as five to ten venues in a single evening – even though he holds down a fulltime job during the week. The man is a machine and he never sleeps!

There aren’t many out there who have nailed club photography quite as good as Ashley, and if you’ve checked out any of his galleries then you’d agree that his work really captures the vibe of a club night, party or festival. And recently, he reached a massive milestone when he submitted his 1,000th gallery for inthemix! It’s a massive achievement, and Angus Paterson from ITM speaks to him to get the skinny on what he’s been up to.

You’ve just reached your 1,000th gallery for ITM. That’s a massive contribution you’ve made to the site! Tell us how you first got involved taking photos for ITM.

A friend of mine first introduced me to ITM back in late 2003 and I signed up as a contributor in early 2004. I decided to get involved as saw it as a great way to meet people who had the same musical taste as I had.

What got you excited about club photography in the first place? Is it the music, the people or the vibe that keeps bringing you back to capture it time after time?

What drew me to club photography in the first place was the people. Then I found it inspiring to capture the culture of dance music in Sydney.

How do you feel about your work a couple of years down the track? You’ve obviously improved a lot, are you proud of a lot of the work you’ve done?

I can see my work has improved since I first started shooting. I remember my first gig was at the Gaelic Club for a drum n’ bass night organized by the 50/50 crew. Back then there were no digital SLR cameras so I used a digital compact camera and a film camera. Over the years with a little bit of help with technology I can see that my work has improved since I first started.

You’re known for visiting 5 to 10 clubs in a single evening, often as many as four nights a week. You also hold down a full-time job during the week. How do you keep your energy up?

Some of this comes down to good planning, and I guess my body is used to going out so I’ve become immune to the late nights. Also having lots of energy drinks like Red Bull does help, I think I should get some sort of sponsorship deal with them.

In the words of an experienced pro, what do you think it takes to capture the vibe of a club or event? Anything you go looking for in particular?

Definitely the music helps, it’s the DJ that I rely on to create the vibe of the club, if the DJ is bringing out quality tracks then it helps to get people get out of their shells, get loose and have a good time. I don’t particularly look for pretty people in my photos; I look for people who are interesting.

What do you consider it more important to capture: the punters, the artists or the overall vibe of the evening?

I think capturing the overall vibe is most important. What I try to show to people who haven’t visited the club is a feeling like they have been in the club, I try to capture the essence of the music through the photos.

What are some of the most memorable nights that you’ve photographed?

One of the most memorable nights was when DJ Tiesto played at Home Nightclub for the Heineken Thirst event. I’ve never seen Home nightclub that packed before. There was incredible vibe in the club as everyone wanted to see Tiesto, it was an insane night as everyone there wanted a piece of him, everyone was dancing as one.

Earlier this year your work was featured in an exhibition focusing on photography taken at the Purple Sneakers night. That’s definitely one of your regular events, what is it that you like so much about that night?

Purple Sneakers is dynamic, it’s fun, it’s outrageous and it’s one of a kind. I haven’t seen anything else in Sydney that has the same sort of vibe as Purple Sneakers, full credit to the boundary sounds crew for thinking outside the square and creating something different, rather than following the same sort of club night.

You’ve been in a lot of clubs over the years. Any particularly funny stories you could tell us, hilarious stories or funny things you’ve seen?

One funny experience I’ve had, that I’ve found amusing has to do with a certain t-shirt I’ve worn, the famous ‘I love Kimberley’ shirt [ Kimberley is one of Sydney’s most notorious ITM forum spammers, for those not in the know ]. I remember wearing it at a big festival, but since then I’ve had so many people come up to me saying I’m Kimberley’s boyfriend, which I’m not.

Recently you’ve also been growing your profile as a rock photographer, doing a lot of work for websites like www.fasterlouder.com.au. What do you see as the chief differences between rock and club photography, and what do you prefer?

Rock photography is certainly more harder and more competitive. You are often only given three songs to capture a moment, with no flash and whatever available light there is. Club photography is more relax and less pressured to take the photos. Both have certain qualities that I enjoy, so I like both.

Any plans to take it professional in the near future?

Yes I would love to, but I feel I still have a lot to learn.

Any special message to the punters who check out your photos on ITM regularly?

Thanks for looking at my photos and I can’t wait to meet you on the dance floor.

Ashley Mar is still amongst it so you’ll be able to spot him out in Sydney clubs this weekend, and if you wanna take a squiz at any of his 1,000+ galleries then you can check out his personal profile. ITM extends a massive congratulations to Ash and looks forward to more excellent photography from him in the future!
