Meet Producer & DJ John Dimas

Angus Paterson · 21 July 2014 · Ibiza Voice

There’s a nice anecdote from the beginning of the career of Greece-born John Dimas; it goes that his love for club music sprang from school project that saw him constructing his own 3-channel mixer. While his school lab held onto that particular artifact from his early days, it was the launchpad for what’s evolved since then into a natural affinity for house music.

Focusing on collaborations early in his career, for the last several years he’s been responsible for a steady stream of releases like La Vie En Rose, Claap and One Records. There’s a rather distinct sonic quality to all of his work; drawn from his natural affinity with the earthy, jackin’ charms of classic US house music, it’s all given a modern edge that is completely distinct.

Looking to the summer season, the major development in Dimas’ career this year will be his first ever visits to Ibiza, which will include several dates at the lauded Circoloco residency at DC10.

In preparation for the first of the shows, I Voice caught up with Dimas to find out what’s cooking.

What have been the year’s major developments for you so far?
So far moving to Lola ED agency early this year is one of them; finally releasing Hypnotize Me on One Records is another one, a track that Adam Shelton and Subb-An have been playing for 2 years. And last but not least, debuting at Circoloco at DC10 in Ibiza, the infamous Monday sessions. First date is on Monday 21st July, then 25th August and last 15th September 2014.

Obviously the Ibiza season is now in full swing, and your Circoloco dates are a big deal. I know it’s your first time playing in Ibiza; how did this come about?
Well it’s my first time in Ibiza, not only to play, but also in general. I’ve never headed yet to the White Island. Of course I heard a lot of amazing words about DC10 from my friends who’ve played there. But honestly I didn’t expect to be spinning there yet, and as well not one but three dates are locked in. I’m so excited about it… whoaa I can’t wait!

A development for you has been the shift from collaborative work under the Actor One project, towards a larger focus on solo work. What led to this do you think?
I would say we had too different tastes in music. We tried to develop another collaboration, but it didn’t work at the end. Then we stopped after 1 year, and I started focusing on my solo productions.

Last year was also a period that you seemed to step things up a little in terms of studio work, and the amount of releases you were putting out.
Last year I did three EPs and a few remixes. More or less all these tracks, especially the originals, were pretty much done the summer before.

Indeed, I like to work 6 months ahead, so that once I have finished my work I can have time for digging some music.

You’ve had relationships with a number of different record labels over the years. What’s your strategy at the moment, in terms of how you put your music out?
Yes that’s true, this is some kind of a strategy. I prefer to have two or three ‘home’ labels, usually friends’ labels, like La Vie En Rose, One Records and so forth.., so I can focus on releasing with them, instead of releasing everywhere. Also I prefer to keep releases to three a year, or four at maximum.

Lastly, I’m trying to avoid the digital-only release, as I think music can get often lost in the big digital ocean. Especially on Beatport, where thousands of tracks are released across so many genres, it’s hard to stand out. Matter of fact, all of my EPs that I have released been presented in both formats.

One of the defining aspects of your work as a producer is your affinity with the classic US sounds; though you bring a very modern approach in terms of the techno edge to things. How would you define the intersection between ‘classic’ and’ ‘modern’?
I’m just trying to draw inspiration from this music I’ve heard and played during my years of DJing. And for me, the only way to do that is to make tracks based on older stuff, and then to make them sound more fresh, or modern.

You know, at the end of the day we all play classics, so I’m trying to do the opposite.

As an artist, you have an interesting mix of national identity; you hail form Greece, you now reside in Germany and you also have a natural affinity with US sounds. How do all these different influences pull together in your music?
I don’t think it really matters where you are from, or where you live, in terms of how you express yourself. You get inspired of course by the city that you live in, especially if it is a city with a big music scene.

However, I’ve lived in Berlin for 2 years, and before that I was making my music from Greece, and there I was already inspired.

What sort of influence has relocating to Berlin had on your career?
I’m living in Berlin for just 2 years now, and living there makes things easier, that’s for sure. For example, meeting other artists is simpler, as is being able to listen to DJs that you like more often. The clubs are so good too; overall, it has been a great and positive influence.

Do you feel you still have a strong connection with the underground scene back home?
I play in Greece once a year more or less. Personally I believe being miles away ruins these kinds of connections. So I’d say sadly I’m no longer that connected to the local underground scene.

Finally, what’s on the agenda for the rest of the year?
I have two upcoming EPs. One is due mid September on Taverna Tracks, a brand new label from a friend in Berlin, and the second on Overall Music in November. In addition, there will be a few remixes coming out on Silver Network, Djebali Reworks Series, Turquoise Blue, and System2. Also Berliners, Look out too for the One Records label night happening at Watergate on 16th of August.
In the meantime, I’ll see you on Monday at DC10!
