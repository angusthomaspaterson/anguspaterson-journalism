Oxia: "I brought the night alive again & relived my sentiments of when I played the club"

Angus Paterson · 17 December 2014 · Ibiza Voice

French DJ/producer Olivier Raymond AKA Oxia offers one of the textbook examples of house and techno consistency; along with a few giddy peaks in his career of course. Involved since the early 90s, it was actually the groundbreaking ‘Domino’ on Kompakt in 2006 that proved his seminal record. Largely though, it’s been a consistent approach to quality that has defined Oxia; something that on display with his new mix compilation ‘Kyō 001’.

Curated to reflect his residency at the Singapore club of the same name, ‘Kyō 001’ is arguably one of the best mixes of the year on the groovier, tech house side of things, delivering a smooth ride from the tougher, groovier records through to the more melodic and deeper selections.

“I brought the night alive again, and relived my sentiments of when I played the club,” Oxia told I Voice of the compilation. “This means playing relatively open, groovy but also more melodic and melancholic. I constructed the mix a bit as how a night evolves: deep enough at the start, stepping up gear, and come back down at the end of the night.”

I Voice spoke to Oxia to find out about the process that went into drawing ‘Kyō 001’ together, as well as what else he’s been up to recently.

How has the year been treating you, and what have the standouts been?
It has been a rather good year actually. My releases were all well received and worked well on the floor, and sales too. I played amazing gigs. Yeah, looking back at 2014, there were loads of good things. It is difficult to pick one or two. But one of those highlights was certainly playing one of, if not the, biggest nights on Ibiza. ‘Music On’ at Amnesia, and this twice and seeing new ‘Music On’ dates in the 2015 diary already.

A few months after my debut at the club, the people behind Kyo spoke me about this project they had in mind: starting the Kyo mix CD series. We had from the beginning a really good feeling between us all, so it was actually a natural process...You’ve got a rather sexy compilation out in the form of ‘Kyō 001’. How did you get involved with mixing this compilation?
I played twice at Kyo since the opening about a year and a half ago. A few months after my debut at the club, the people behind Kyo spoke me about this project they had in mind: starting the Kyo mix CD series. We had from the beginning a really good feeling between us all, so it was actually a natural process.

I felt the vibe of the club immediately, as well as its night ‘FORWARD’, so I also accepted this invitation with pleasure. Originally the mix would be released at the beginning of 2014, but it has been pushed back, which is often the case with a first release. And maybe for the best, cause in the mean time I could also play their first anniversary bash, together with my friend Nicolas Masseyeff. It was a beautiful night!

There’s a great mix of labels showcased in the mix; Poker Flat, Mobilee, Saved, Moon Harbour and beyond. What was process you went through in selecting and licensing?
I listened to a lot of music. It’s difficult only choosing 20 tracks. Originally I had far more of course, and then I started to play and try different things to finally arrive at the mix as it is. I made the tracklisting, but as the release date got pushed back I ended up remaking it, while also keeping a few tracks. It also takes up quite a bit of time. Selecting, making the licensing request, waiting for the approval, etc. I had carte blanche of course as well. I made the mix as how I feel it, playing what I like, while also thinking of the vibe and sentiment of Kyo club.

It’s always a bit of a challenge constructing a mix with different styles, but have it remain a coherent session... There’s nice movement in the mix from the tougher, groovy records in the beginning, to the more melodic and deeper selections. What was your aim in terms of programming?
It’s always a bit of a challenge constructing a mix with different styles, but have it remain a coherent session. As mentioned previously I tried out different combinations, orders.

I also made some edits of tracks to get where I wanted to get. For me this mix needed to feel as if it was a track itself: having it evolve, some surprising changes…

You’ve had a strong presence in French house and techno stretching back to the 90s. What points in your career stick out for you?
Oh, there are multiple reasons why I am still present. To start with, I still have the same passion as in the beginning, and that is very important I think! I invest enough in this too. I stay honest, and I always did what I loved doing, without too much thinking if it was ‘fashionable’ enough or not. Several periods were of great importance. My first EP in ‘95, followed by the first releases on foreign labels, and of course releasing on Intec in 2000. It was from that point on I also started DJing a lot outside France. And my first album! It’s always a major step. Of course ‘Domino’ on Kompakt in 2006 and ‘Whole Life’ in 2009 on 8Bit, which were both big tracks worldwide. My second album, which was difficult as people have certain expectations. The feedback was really great though. I was so happy that I did different things and made non-dancefloor tracks, worked with singers, etc.

OXIA - Tracks & Remixes

You last LP ‘Tides of Mind’ was in 2012. What has your focus been in the studio since then, and what do you have planned for what is coming up?
Since my album I made numerous remixes and this year I did an EP on SAVED, and there is another one coming up in December, an EP on Material, and I did a remix for Visionquest and a collaboration with Agoria, but also remixes for Moan and Clarisse. Much more coming up!

What’s essential is to play the music you like and love, if this is house or techno or electro or whatever genre...Kyō 001’ is a good example of keeping apace with trends in house and techno. Would you say you find this easy, or do trends swing in and out of what you can relate with?
Whether it’s a hype or not, trendy or not, when I like a track I play it. I play the music I play because I like it, not because it’s ‘in’. Now unconsciously we are all a little influenced by hype, one way or another. What’s essential is to play the music you like and love, if this is house or techno or electro or whatever genre. In over 20 years now, I listened and played different genres, related to what was happening in my own personal life. There are times I’ll be doing and playing different, I don’t like to stay forever and ever in the same corner, and this is something very natural.

Interestingly enough, I read a DJ saying in an interview recently that he’d pulled out ‘Domino’ this year again over the Ibiza season; it’s proved quite seminal.
True, this year the track got played quite a lot again by several DJ’s. They tell me often it’s a classic, a uniting track, and I am very proud and happy to have been able making such a track in my career. A production that has left something behind with people. Many people I talk to, tell me they ‘discovered’ techno through this EP and it was their first introduction. I must admit there are times I kind of had enough of it, and people keeps asking to play the track but in the end I can actually only be happy!
