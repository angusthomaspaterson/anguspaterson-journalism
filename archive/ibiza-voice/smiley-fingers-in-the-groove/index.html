Smiley Fingers... In The Groove

Angus Paterson · 6 August 2014 · Ibiza Voice

Around 5 years ago, Larry Cadge was feeling burnt out after working for years in the dance industry. A militant, stripped-back sound had taken hold of underground house and techno, and he wasn’t feeling it. Interestingly enough, it was this period of doubt that led to the establishment of his Smiley Fingers record label, as well as helping him define its identity.

Kicking things off with an EP from label regular Rick Sander in 2010, the Smiley Fingers sound was a mix of tight tech house groves, as well as the more melodic, uplifting and fun elements associated with house music. It was an ethos reflected in the various smiley emoticons that were used to visually represent the sunnier, less serious identity of the label.

With more than 10 releases under their belt, Smiley Fingers started off the year in style in the form of a big mainroom hit with Larry Cadge ‘Vicious Circle’. And with Tapesh & Dayne S How I Do making an impact in the opening months of the Ibiza summer season, we thought it was time to sit down with Smiley Fingers for an ‘In The Groove’ profile.

How has the year been going for the label so far?
This seems an interesting year for us. In February we released Larry Cadge ‘Vicious Circle’, it was a real surprise for us, it reached the Top 10 in Tech House chart and was supported by DJs like Solomun, Marco Carola, Martin Eyerer, Roger Sanchez, Tiesto, Ray Okpara and many others…

In March ‘How I Do’ entered the Top 10 Indie/Nu Disco Chart on Beatport, and was the 4th most played track in Ibiza according to Shazam. Then we released our first Sample Library, ‘Larry Cadge - Smiley Fingers Sounds Vol 1.’ After all the energies we spent to celebrate our 100th release the last October, we didn’t expected to start the New Year this way!

Smiley Fingers has now been operating for around 5 years. What led you to establishing the label, and what was your initial intention in terms of what you were trying to achieve?
After many years behind the scenes of the music industry, I took some time off from work. I travelled a lot, visiting many countries and playing gigs in the weirdest places. In 2009 it was during the era of the minimal techno, and I was not into it.

The logo was born from a funny game... This is our attitude. We proposed something different from to the “serious and dark” mood used by most of the other label...With the experience gained during these years, I was looking to express my idea of dancefloor music; something still in tech house territory, but influenced by soul and funk feelings, that could work on the dancefloor, and from this need the Smiley Fingers label was born. I created the Smiley Fingers’ logo inspired by my son, while painting my fingers with funny faces to make him smile.

The label’s first release was in 2010. Later, during a summer festival, I met Adam Helder, my business partner and together we plan and share all the matters about the label. I’m really satisfied of the results reached during these years but I’m just at the beginning of a long way.

What have been your biggest achievements during that time?
In 2010 one of our first success was ‘Ah Uha’, played by Marco Carola on BBC Radio One, then with the first volume of our sampler ‘We Are Smiley’, magazines like iDj started talking about us.

In 2011 for the first time, we climbed Beatport’s Top 100 chart, we stood there for a few months. From then on DJs playing Smiley Fingers’ tracks gradually increased, it was amazing!




I’d identify the ‘Smiley Fingers sound’ as a balanced mix of tough grooves and uplifting melodies. How would you define the vibe of your releases yourself?
You’re right! Our aim is to select quality music where tech house and deep, dark techno collide.

We can describe our sound as a party-hearty tech house influenced by a melodic attitude, sometimes moving towards darker and dubbier atmospheres.

With so many house labels out there, how were you looking to stand out initially and establish your musical identity? And how has this evolved over the years?
It’s always difficult to find tracks that perfectly match the sound we have in mind. Identity is important, but sometimes we receive tracks that even though they are not 100% our sound, they are just too good to be rejected, and we decide to release anyway, only because they transmit us a good feeling. For instance ‘Pathway To A Dream’ released in June, that will be followed by some remixes in September.

Rick Sanders has been one of the label’s most regular producers since the early days. Did you see him as one of the label’s priorities?
Yes, I met Rick when he was producing music just for fun, uploading his tracks on YouTube. I believed in his talent from the very beginning, he signed his very first release with us.

On Smiley Fingers he released success tracks as ‘We Shall See’, ‘Turn it Up’ and ‘Queen’. With tracks like ‘Make It Free’, he has expressed his potentiality, I'm sure you will hear a lot about him!

One of your stated aims was supporting and developing new and upcoming artists. Who are some of the other key producers you’ve worked with?
We have many interesting artists on Smiley, and Adam and I did a big job of selecting some of the hottest talents, choosing from the tons of demos we receive every month.

Among those we found Danny O, resident at Mambo and other great venues in Ibiza, Technophile who is an interesting DJ/producer from Lebanon, member of Ronin & Nesta duo and owner of Uberhaus Club.

Recently we released an EP by Daniele Stella, an inspired artist with a great feeling for the melody. Jobb & Corco, a young French duo with a fresh deep/tech house sound, that have already released on Material and Circle Music. Last year, during the ADE we met Metrophonique, an Italian duo that will go out with a great release in August. Also, our sub-label Smiley Fingers Limited is dedicated to emerging artists.

You’ve said our visual identity was inspired by “emoticons typical of the Internet era”. It’s a rather positive visual branding for the label. What were you trying to achieve here?
Yes, as I told you the logo was born from a funny game... This is our attitude. We proposed something different from to the “serious and dark” mood used by most of the other labels… So we try to transmit good vibes and make people smile, with our sound, artworks and logo.

You talk in your bio about taking “a progressive approach towards the digital industry.” Smiley Fingers began in a music industry that had been well and truly transformed by digital distribution. How has this defined your approach as a record label?
Digital made easier to manage a label, from getting demos, to publishing and promoting new releases, though the downside is this leads to the birth of lots of new labels, most of which don't last more than a few years.

The music scene nowadays is very inflated and there is a lot of competition, so we’re trying to build a solid fan base that love our sound and our brand.

Beside them we have a large group of professionals like DJs, radio shows, magazines and blogs supporting us, and we are supported also by Deejayfeedback that takes care of our promotion.

What’s planned for the label in the next 12 months?
We will release more Sample Packs, and the next will be by Rick Sanders, followed by the other guys in our roster. It’s nice to think that other artists could put a little of Smiley’s sound in their own productions. We are also planning release in some very limited vinyl editions and scheduling label showcases.
