Oliver Huntemann: “The idea is to capture a night in the club, like a photo in an album.”

Angus Paterson · 27 November 2014 · Ibiza Voice

Hamburg veteran Oliver Huntemann captures with his ‘PLAY!’ series that authentic feeling of what it’s like to get lost in a club for an evening; at least, it’s as close as you’ll get with listening on your home stereo. How the bass reverberates, how the energy resonates in the crowd, the way they respond to each different twist and turn of the set; It’s an unmistakable energy that you just don’t get with a studio-prepared mix.

“The idea behind it is to capture a night in the club, one on one,” Huntemann told I Voice. “How it was, to have a memory. Like a picture in a family photo album. With the ups and downs, emotions, feelings and interactions of the crowd. It’s like a snapshot, and that’s the thrill of this project. The concept stays the same, but the music changes and grows with me and my tastes.”

With his latest installment ‘PLAY! 05 [live] Vienna/Austria’, Huntemann does a sterling job of bottling his polished ability to keep a club in his thrall. There’s a proper journey across the three hours of the two discs, beginning with the groovier selections before before transitioning into the more slamming techno in its second half. And the energy of the night is captured with crisp precision; every whoop, every shout and every fist-pump.

You’ll find Huntemann’s massive new main room weapon ‘Vienna’ on ‘PLAY! 05’, and it also represents the first major release on the brand new Senso Sounds label, his new venture after his Ideal Audio label was folded earlier this year. IVoice spoke to him to find out more.

What is the process behind preparing a release like ‘PLAY! 05’?
It’s a DJ set, recorded live in the best club of a major city anywhere in the world, and this time I choose Grelle Forelle in Vienna. It’s a process we work on for about half a year. Once I’ve decided where I’ll record the next edition, and the negotiation with the club is successful, we start preparing everything else. The architecture of the tracks is quite easy, I write down a list of tracks I’d like to include on the mix. The administrator of my label licenses the tracks. I speak to befriended producers about the exclusive tracks. Once all is approved then I have a list of tracks, which I build my mainbox for the recording and then I can play them in a total freedom, interacting with the crowd or in a dramatic order that I think is right for the night.

There is lot of preparation from the club side, and it can’t be done in every venue.
We need sound engineers to prepare the club by hanging microphones to record the atmosphere...There is lot of preparation from the club side, and it can’t be done in every venue. We need sound engineers to prepare the club by hanging microphones to record the atmosphere. I have the atmosphere and audio on separate channels, and them mix them in the right balance. Luckily all my recordings went well so far, and I haven’t been disappointed yet. You have a more nerves than a typical gig, but it’s all in balance.

While we have access to so many live sets on the web nowadays, it seems there is something still quite special about a curated live CD.
I believe in the motto of "class not mass". Everything is available on the web. If I would think or worry too much about that, I would quit working right away. I like doing special things and releasing the ‘PLAY!’ compilation is like a thank you to all my fans that still love having a physical product in their hands. It’s more personal than just another podcast, and it tightens the relationship between artist and fan. I like the haptic perception when you put the CD for the first time in the player, while you browse the booklet, check out the words and playlists or look at the pictures.

I believe more in the interaction with the crowd, the right dramatic flow and track selection. It’s all about time and spirit...Dance lovers are quite indulged with all the 5+ hour extended sets available to them. Is there a limitation to only having 80 minutes to work with on each CD?
We are talking about a 2xCD, which provides almost three hours of music. That 5 hours thing sounds always nice with the big story but, if you tell your story, how do you include your audience in that? I believe more in the interaction with the crowd, the right dramatic flow and track selection. It’s all about time and spirit. I record a live mix and give it back one to one. 160 minutes full of drama and pounding dark techno is perfect for a listening session, at home or in the car. For longer sets you need the experience in the club. Myself, I love to do all-night-long sets from time to time, but to be honest, only a few people come at the very beginning and stay till the end, so it seems not too many are interested in five to ten hour sets.

The last time you were speaking to press a lot was around the release of your “Fünf” compilation for Ideal Audio. How did that project play out for you?
I was very happy with the result and the according tour. Talking about Ideal Audio, I have to mention that I’ve stopped running the labels Confused and Ideal Audio due to the split with my long-time business partner Jan Langer from Plantage13, so I’m focusing on my new imprint Senso Sounds now. So, no big developments for Ideal Audio in this case, unless it gets famous after its death.

I don’t follow trends much, but what I’ve seen is that techno has come back with fresh sounds and energy. This is the logical counter movement to the deep house overkill last year...What have some of the highlights been for you in 2014?
The biggest highlight of the year was the birth of my new label Senso Sounds, and the booking agency Kontrast Artists. After a long time where all the parts of my business have been spread across different cities, I decided to bring it all together. I now have one top team working in the office in Hamburg on all my ideas and artistic concerns. I don’t follow trends much, but what I’ve seen is that techno has come back with fresh sounds and energy. This is the logical counter movement to the deep house overkill last year.

What is the mission statement for Senso Sounds?
I simply felt the need to begin something new. A label has an image and sometimes it’s easier to start all over than refreshing old things. While Ideal was managed by me from Hamburg and administrated by Jan from Bremen, I felt on a certain point that I’d like to have everything in my hands. That’s the reason why I founded Senso Sounds. We have people working only for Senso Sounds in the office, and all side works such as graphics, distribution and so on are based in Hamburg too. This is really a benefit to make every release special and unique. I always dreamed about having a team only working on my vision, and that happens now. It doesn’t differ sound wise that much. It’s not a label that I founded because I thought: “Oh okay, techno is cool, but I’d like to have a house label as well, to cover all genres!” It’s a consequent follow up of what I’ve been doing in the last years, and of what I want to do in the future.

There’s an ongoing discussion as to how underground club culture is being impacted by the commercialization we’ve seen the past few years. As someone with an extensive history in dance music, what are your insights in 2014?
I don’t have this discussion in my surroundings. This “EDM is evil” story is a bit boring, isn’t it? Actually, I’m quite happy that electronic music in all its forms is so successful nowadays. Underground and overground, I believe it all belongs together. The underground provides the ideas and creativity, and the mainstream EDM does the big business. Electronic music wouldn’t exits anymore without all these facets. Each to their own. This discussion has existed since techno and house became bigger in the early 90s, and did it have any negative impact? No, because we are still listening and dancing to electronic music in all its varieties after 25 years.
