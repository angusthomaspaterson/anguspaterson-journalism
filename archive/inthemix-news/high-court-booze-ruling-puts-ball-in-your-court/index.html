High Court booze ruling puts ball in your court

Angus Paterson · 11 November 2009 · inthemix

As debate continues across the country over alcohol-related violence and 2am lockouts, a Federal High Court ruling this week has shifted the responsibility for drunken behaviour away from pubs and clubs, and back onto the punters themselves. The controversial decision has ruled that venues have no general duty of care to protect patrons from the consequences of getting drunk, swinging it back over to the individual with the assertion that it’s fairer that “the drinker, rather than the seller of drink,” should be held liable in the case of injury or other misfortune.

The trial was an appeal from a Tasmanian venue against an earlier ruling from their state’s Supreme Court, which had held the venue responsible for the car accident death of an intoxicated patron, after it had returned motorcycle keys to the victim shortly before the crash. However, all five judges on the High Court bench ruled on Tuesday that the State Court’s decision should be overturned.

The court concluded that, “save in exceptional circumstances, publicans owe no duty of care to their customers in relation to how much alcohol is served.” Justices Gummow, Heydon and Crennan asserted emphasised their ruling as the most sensible approach, because “the opposite view would create enormous difficulties … relating to customer autonomy and coherence with legal norms… Expressions like ‘intoxication’, ‘inebriation’ and ‘drunkenness’ are difficult to both define and to apply.”

The Australian reports that the ruling has been greeted less than enthusiastically by health professionals, who were worried the change could undermine the responsible service of alcohol by pubs and clubs. The Australian Hotels Association on the other hand has hailed the ruling as sending a firm message to drinkers they need to take responsibility for their own actions, while at the same time cautioning that it shouldn’t be seen by punters as a green card to “get plastered” when out and about.

Could this be the sign of things to come with pubs and clubs across the country? A precedent perhaps, that will mean that well-behaved majority are no longer punished for the anti-social behaviour of a select few? We can only hope.
