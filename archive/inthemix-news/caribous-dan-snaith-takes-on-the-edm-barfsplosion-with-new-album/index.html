Caribou's Dan Snaith takes on the ‘EDM barfsplosion’ with new album

Angus Paterson · 26 July 2012 · inthemix

Caribou producer Dan Snaith has been less than impressed with dance music’s recent shift away from exploring uncharted territory, towards an industry-sanctioned hit factory, so much that he’s put the Daphni moniker to use for his upcoming album Jiaolong; his own cheeky moniker for electronic dance productions, to counter the dreaded and ubiquitous ‘EDM’.

According to Snaith, the new project was inspired by those genuine “transcendent moments” that’ll be familiar to the clubbers out to discover something genuinely new and exciting when they venture out to listen to electronic music. “I’d fallen back in love with moments in small, dark clubs when a DJ puts on a piece of music that not only can you not identify but that until you heard it, you could not have conceived of it existing,” Snaith said in a statement this week.

“Set against the backdrop of bland and functional dance music and the mind-numbing predictability of the EDM barfsplosion currently gripping the corporate ravesters, there is a small world where dance music lives up to its potential to liberate, surprise, and innovate. It’s there that I hope Daphni has a place.”

However you judge Snaith’s decision to launch a project in direct conflict with some of the recent cultural movements in dance music, and whether you see the point in it, true lovers of clubbing culture will find plenty to identify in with what’s being said.

“In contrast to live concerts where bands predictably string together the songs from their most recent album, DJs have the potential to blindside you, be genuinely surprising. As a music producer, the parameters of dance music seem wider. The rhythmic underpinnings are liberating rather than constraining, allowing the rest of the elements to coalesce from a broad palette.” Daphni’s Jiaolong is out October 8 in Australia via Jiaolong.
