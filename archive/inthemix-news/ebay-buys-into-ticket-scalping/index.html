eBay buys into ticket scalping

Angus Paterson · 11 January 2007 · inthemix

It was less than a month ago that eBay took the organisers of the Big Day Out to court, with the online auction house delivering a Federal Court smackdown and preventing the promoters from taking any further action to halt ticket scalping. It seems this legal action was part of a grander plan to secure its status as a premium online ticket reseller, following the news it has thrown down a whopping $396 million to purchase the burgeoning US online ticket broker StubHub Inc.

The deal is expected to close by the end of April, with eBay claiming the purchase will allow it to expand its presence in the online tickets segment, while also allowing StubHub to leverage the massive resources of its parent company and pursue further growth.

“StubHub has been extremely successful in the online tickets segment, and it’s a perfect complement to eBay’s tickets business,” says Bill Cobb, from eBay in the US. “Together we can strengthen both businesses and provide fans with more choice and better service.”

Happy days indeed, but the approach taken by StubHub to ticket reselling has gathered a mixed response. Unlike the current arrangement under eBay, all tickets sold on the site are actually guaranteed to be authentic and according to the Sydney Morning Herald, the approach has won favour from more than 30 sports teams in the US that actually guide fans to the service. But the company has been subject to just as much strident criticism from teams who take issue with it reselling tickets at price significantly higher than the original market value.

Stay tuned to ITM for further developments, of how the sale will impact music fans in Australia and any possible plans of eBay to expand StubHub outside of the US.
