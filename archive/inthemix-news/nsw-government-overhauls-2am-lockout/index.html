NSW Government overhauls 2am lockout

Angus Paterson · 10 September 2009 · inthemix

Lockouts are back on the agenda again for New South Wales punters, with Premier Nathan Rees yesterday unveiling a new-look safety regime to address alcohol related violence in NSW pubs and clubs, however the news is surprisingly rosy for clubbers. None of our preferred venues will be subject to a 2am lockout once the new system comes into place in early December, with only a handful of clubs subject to any kind of conditions at all.

“The community is sick and tired of being subjected to violence and these new arrangements will continue to target pubs and clubs that have rising alcohol-related assault rates,” Rees said in a statement released yesterday. “Three new categories based on assaults will replace the current system, and reclassification of venues will be possible twice a year based on a review of assault data.”

The ‘high-risk’ venues – with 19 or more assaults a year – will be subject to similar conditions that have already been in force over the past year at the pubs and clubs deemed ‘most violent’ when amendments were made to the NSW Licence Conditions last November. These include a 2am lockout, no glasses allowed after midnight, a ban on shots, doubles and RTDs over 5%, along with other conditions. For ‘level 2’ and ‘level 3’ venues – between 12-to-18 and 8-to-11 recorded assaults respectively – the penalties are a lot less severe, with both the 2am lockout and the ban on shots lifted.

Looking at the specific venues that made it onto the list, according to data released by the NSW Bureau of Crime Statistics and Research (BOCSAR) ranking licensed premises from July 2008 to June 2009, a majority of the clubs most likely to be favored by ITMers have been thankfully spared from any mention. However, there’s a few notable exceptions where venues have been slapped with ‘level 2’ and ‘level 3’ restrictions. Kings Cross venue Dragonfly ranks #64 on the list with 13 assaults, along with Candy’s Apartment, The Bourbon and The Eastern at equal #79 with 12 assaults, while Oxford Street haunts Arq, Havana and Oxford Art Factory are at equal #97 place with 11 assaults. With nearly 150 venues rating a mention on the list, things could be a lot worse for NSW clubbers.

A spokesperson from the Premier’s Department has told ITM that the venues that were previously subject to a 2am lockout, including Home Nightclub and ARQ, have yet to be removed from the list. “The data [used] will be compiled on December 1 and June 1 each year using 12 months of rolling data. Venues added to the list will be subject to the special conditions for a minimum of 6 months,” said Rees.
