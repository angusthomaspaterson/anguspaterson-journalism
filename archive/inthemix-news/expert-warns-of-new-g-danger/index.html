Expert warns of new G danger

Angus Paterson · 20 May 2009 · inthemix

Do you know anyone who takes, or is planning on taking GHB? Well, they could very well be taking a completely different substance known as GBL, according to drug expert Paul Dillon. He’s claimed the vast majority of GBH that’s sold on the streets is actually gamma-butyrolactone, or GBL, a substance that’s commonly used as a paint stripper, rust remover or industrial solvent. When ingested by the body, the chemical converts to the better known chemical gamma-hydroxybutyrate, or GHB.

Paul Dillon, director for Drug and Alcohol Research and Training Australia, told ninemsn this week that many punters who think they are taking GHB are in fact unknowingly ingesting GBL. “Dealers stopped selling GHB because if you get caught with it you will get prosecuted, whereas with GBL it’s a little bit trickier to police,” he said. GBL is currently freely available because of its legal use, but police have the power to charge dealers and users if they’re identified for “non-sanctioned” use.

Dillon also claims that the use of GBL brings with it a whole new set of dangers. “It takes a little bit longer to work than GHB because of the conversion process, so people tend to take more because they haven’t started to feel the effects. This is potentially very dangerous and can lead to more overdoses.”

However, whether you’re using GHB or GBL, both are drugs that are highly dangerous and bring potentially fatal consequences. “They are very risky drugs because they are highly dose dependent. The difference between having a good time and ending up in hospital with your entire respiratory system in collapse is quite slim.”
