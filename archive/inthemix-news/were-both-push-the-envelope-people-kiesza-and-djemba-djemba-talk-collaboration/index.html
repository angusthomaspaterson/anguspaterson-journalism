“We’re both ‘push the envelope’ people”: Kiesza and Djemba Djemba talk collaboration

Angus Paterson · 24 November 2015 · inthemix

If necessity is the mother of invention, then collaboration is the hot mistress: from making a track to mixing a drink, it all revolves around collaboration. What we borrow, what we share, what we steal, #WhatWeBring. We’ve teamed up with Smirnoff for a series of articles exploring the world of international collaboration.

From Diplo and Skrillex’s chart-topping duo Jack Ü to Ferry Corsten and Markus Schulz’s trance titan team-up New World Punx, dance music thrives in the fertile world of collaboration.

Smirnoff has reached out for a little bit of that mojo with their international “What We Bring” movement, which sees dance/pop star Kiesza team up with rising LA bass producer (and Mad Decent protégé) Djemba Djemba (AKA Andrew Swanson) to create the cross-over track Give It To The Moment, which will be remixed by a raft of fast-rising producers from around the world.

Kiesza and Swanson spoke with inthemix about the shrinking divide between club music and the mainstream, and the potency of sharing skills in the studio.

Following in the footsteps of Jack Ü’s chart-topper Where Are Ü Now, Kiesza and Swanson’s collaboration Give It To The Moment takes sounds you’d normally only find in the club – or on SoundCloud – and packages them up for mass radio consumption.

“I definitely like that it’s different from what you’d normally describe as trap music, or even generic bass, dubstep or EDM,” Swanson says of the track, on the phone from Los Angeles. “I wanted to try something new, but often it’s tough when you do something [different] with a vocalist, because usually they’re scared of the sound. But things are changing, and I think Kiesza is one of those artists who are really open to new sounds, so it just kind of worked…

“The drop was actually her voice…and we did it before the whole Justin Bieber thing. She just sang it in the booth, she was like, ‘what if the drop was like [hums melody]?’ I’d been recording it, so I just basically chopped it up and put it in the session.”

“I think as artists, we’re both ‘push the envelope’ kinds of people,” Kiesza adds. “When we got into the room together, it ended up being the most experimental thing ever.”

Kiesza broke into the pop and dance world’s last year with her single Hideaway, the smash hit with an ear-worm bassline, her powerhouse vocals, and the signature single-shot film clip that’s racked up more than 270 million YouTube views. As it happens, that breakthrough track coincided with the moment Kiesza found her voice as an artist, after writing everything from jazz and hip hop to death metal.

“Hideaway was the first song where I said screw it, I’m not gonna try to be the same, I’m not gonna try and fit in. I’m just gonna do what makes me happy,” Kiesza says. “So literally the very moment when I stopped worrying about trying to fit in with the rest of the world, that’s what resonated with the rest of the world.”

Since Hideaway hit the charts, Kiesza has worked with a stack of different producers, both on collaborations (like her monster Jack Ü collab’ Take Ü There) and her debut album Sound of a Woman. And the more closely she works with different producers, Kiesza says, the more her songwriting comes to life.

“I can write over beats, but they don’t usually end up being my songs, they’re the songs that I pitch,” she says, referring to her moonlighting gig as a songwriter-for-hire, which has seen her pen tracks for Rihanna, Icona Pop and Kylie Minogue, among others.

“When I started becoming a songwriter, I realised that I loved the creation process. If I was writing something for someone like Rihanna, I’d give my voice more of a raspy sound, a bit more timbre. Sometimes I didn’t know I had these voices in me… [But] what excites me as a musician is creating new styles and sounds…

“If [Swanson] had sent me a beat and I’d written a song, then it’s his beat and my song. But when you’re involved with everything together, me with the beats and him with the song, you have something you wouldn’t have produced separately.”

Swanson has also worked behind the scenes with major pop players like Britney Spears, Chris Brown and Madonna (as well as Major Lazer), but he says the experience of working in the studio with Kiesza, to create a track for Smirnoff’s “What We Bring
“initiative, was one-of-a-kind.

“Normally when you go into a session with a vocalist, you’d be like, ‘alright you’re just doing the vocals’. But Kiesza contributed more than just the vocals – and I brought more than just the beat. I tried to sing in it as well but it just didn’t work out…”

Swanson’s heart may lie in the club, but he says the move into pop production is a “natural” step forward.

“I definitely wanna experiment and make music that appeals to people beyond the EDM side of things…it just so happens my experimental music sounds a little more mainstream right now. That’s nice, so I’m going to take advantage of it. I don’t expect it to last forever.”

“If I’d made this song 10 years ago, people would be like, ‘what the hell is this?’ But dance music has come a long way in the mainstream. Jack Ü have really done a lot, and you can hear it with Disclosure and everybody on the radio these days. It’s youth culture getting older and styles changing. I just happened to be in the right place at the right time…”
