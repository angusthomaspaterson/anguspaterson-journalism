Early-morning violence erupts in Melbourne's clubbing district

Angus Paterson · 14 April 2009 · inthemix

An early-morning brawl that spilled outside into the streets of Melbourne has again ignited debate on alcohol-related violence and late-night partying, after CCTV footage of the 15-person fight was splashed all over the website of the Herald-Sun this morning. The brawl took place at 9.30am on Sunday morning outside a Melbourne bar in Geddes Lane near King Street, with the footage showing punters repeatedly punching each other and hurling bottles at the security guards who were attempting to break up the fight.

The footage is also coupled with another violent incident that was captured at 7.20am on another CCTV camera in Flinders Lane, which shows a man being king-hit from behind a wall, before being hit again as he falls backward to smash his head against the footpath (the Herald-Sun footage can be viewed HERE).

The footage has again put the media spotlight on the nightclub precincts in Melbourne, as it’s the most recent in a spate of incidents that have occurred in the past three months. The city’s Lord Mayor Robert Doyle has spoken to the Herald Sun over the footage, pointing his finger at the venues as the ones who need to be held responsible. “It’s time to look at places where it’s happening and take away their licences. It’s time for us to fight back, and that means unrelenting scrutiny and no tolerance of violence and breaches of licences. We are collecting information through our officers, with police and liquor licensing,” he said.

Doyle has indicated he’ll be looking to bypass the VCAT (Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal) in order to find a speedier solution to shut the venues in question down. “It may be that we need to talk to the Government and police about mechanisms to shut these places down and not allow them to use the legal system to frustrate due process over months and years,” he said. “The answer is not just cutting the numbers of people in there. It’s to shut them down.”

Martha Tsamis, owner of both Inflation nightclub and the Geddes Lounge near where the fight took place, has claimed the brawl did not involve clubbers from her venues, and has insisted that licensees are not solely to blame for violence. Tsamis says that security guards had asked the crowd to leave the precinct shortly before the fight broke out.
