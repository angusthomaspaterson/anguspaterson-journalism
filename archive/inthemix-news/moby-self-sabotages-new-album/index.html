Moby self sabotages new album

Angus Paterson · 17 April 2009 · inthemix

Apparently, we shouldn’t expect anything too earth shaking from the new Moby album. So says Moby himself! This week he launched an apparently self-sabotaging campaign against his upcoming album Wait For Me, revealing he’s tried to focus on making “something that I loved, without really being concerned about how it might be received by the marketplace”.

The new record comes out on Moby’s own Little Idiot Records instead of his usual label EMI, and is “quieter, more mournful and more personal record than some I’ve made in the past”, he promises on its accompanying press release. And writing about it on his always interesting online journal, Moby published a letter he sent to US music industry expert Bob Lefsetz in which he talked of having a “quasi-epiphany” in which he decided to stop “accommodating the market”.

“I had been trying to make myself happy and make radio happy and make the label happy and make press happy and etc, and it made me miserable. And I also don’t really aspire to selling too many records,” the multi-millionaire dance star confessed in his letter. “See, my friends who are writers sell 20,000 books and they’re happy. My friends who are theater directors sell 5,000 tickets during a run and they’re happy,” he added, “I like the idea of humble and reasonable metrics for determining the success of a record.”

Do you believe Moby that his new album aint gonna be worth bothering with? Here at ITM we’re not so sure, because this David Lynch directed clip is making us think otherwise…
