Overdoses responsible for Defqon.1 missing Melbourne

Angus Paterson · 15 April 2009 · inthemix

The Australian branch of iconic Dutch dance promotions company Q-dance, the team that are behind the debut of the massive Defqon.1 Festival in Sydney later this year, have spoken out on why the event will no longer be making an additional stop in Melbourne, pointing to the GHB related overdoses that took place at last December’s event as the reason why they’re not able to bring the brand to the clubbing capital.

“The main factor was that the last Q-dance event had 2,500 people in Melbourne, and during that event eight ambulances were called for people who had overdosed on GHB,” a representative from Q-dance Australia told ITM. “If you staged an event like Defqon.1 for 15,000 people, you would have around 50, 60 or 70 ambulances if you equated that out. Fundamentally, there are just too many people who take GHB at hard music events in Melbourne, and it’s detrimental to the Q-dance brand.”

Q-dance has told ITM that the team are hugely disappointed they’ll no longer be able to bring the Defqon.1 Festival to Melbourne as originally planned, meaning the event will be restricted to a single show in Sydney. The decision to pull out of Melbourne also brings with it a host of other problems for the Q-dance brand. “It’s very challenging to make tours work, because all of your income has to come from the single city… We just want to throw really good parties and share really good music, and to not have that opportunity to do that is very frustrating. Especially given the artists fly all the way from across the world.”

The media attention surrounding drug overdoses and GHB use continues to plague the both the Melbourne dance scene and the promoters working within it, casting the scene in a less than positive light in the mainstream. At least 12 drug-affected party-goers were taken to hospital via ambulance staff at the Q-dance event in late December last year, with around the same number of effected punters taken to hospital by their friends. While there were no arrests, Ambulance and first aid staff pointed to GHB at the time as the source of the hospitalisations.

Health commander for the Metropolitan Ambulance Service Paul Holman spoke to ITM shortly after the event, warning punters of the extreme dangers of GHB. “This is not a feelgood drug. This is a dangerous drug, and it will poison you. You can party hard without these drugs and still enjoy everything the scene has to offer.” Holman also confirmed last December the promoters did everything they could to cooperate with his staff, and provide a safe event. “I’m not sure what else they could have done in this case, they had all he correct safety procedures in place. I’m not critical at all.”
