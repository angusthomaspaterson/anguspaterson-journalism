Move over disco, house music is back

Angus Paterson · 8 January 2009 · inthemix

According to the UK press, at least. ‘Reputable’ rag The Sunday Times accelerated their build ‘em up, knock em down cycle dramatically this week, signaling the death of ‘nouveau disco’ just one week after hailing the Abba-esque genre as London’s Next Big Thing.

“Good riddance o-eight, we’re ready to embrace the shiny new year of nine. Here are some of the things we’re most excited about,” the paper crowed. “HOUSE MUSIC- Four (sic) house parties,” The Times predicted, “It’s the new disco nouveau.”

Louis Vuitton high-top trainers and best-selling relationship manual ‘How To Talk to Girls’ were also included as ‘trends that will define the year ahead’, despite Alec Greven’s book being an unashamed dating guide about how to pick up pre-teen school girls. “It is easy to spot pretty girls because they have big earrings, fancy dresses and all the jewelry,” the 9 year old writes in chapter 3 of his 46 page pamphlet.

“The best choice for most boys is a regular girl,” he advises, “Remember, some pretty girls are coldhearted when it comes to boys. Don’t let them get to you,” he adds.

The Sunday Times also singled out Croatia as ‘the new Ibiza’ (‘Petrcane is attracting all the big names in electronica to its summer festivals’) in stark contrast to the views of leading Croatian promoter Sergej Ludovic. The Zagreb based club chief recently opened new Croatian hotspot Sirup in the capital and also programs Porat, an idyllic outdoor super-club built on a forested hillside by the sea

“Croatia is not the new Ibiza,” Sergej told Skrufff in September last year, following a season in which he’d brought the likes of Secretsundaze, Lee Burridge and LTJ Bukem to the Dalmatian coast. “Ibiza’s developed over decades, we’re different and we want people to discover Croatia for themselves.”

“I will say though that Croatia is becoming a new destination for the people who enjoy the music, clubbing and nature, who don’t want to pay 25 euros for whiskey cola,” he continued, “And with this looming recession, Porat is ready for you people out there to dance under the stars and trees with us. See you in July,” said Sergej.
