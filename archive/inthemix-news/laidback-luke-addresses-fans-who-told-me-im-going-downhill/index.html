Laidback Luke addresses fans who "told me I'm going downhill"

Angus Paterson · 7 June 2013 · inthemix

“I’ve had a bit of a struggle this week,” Dutch powerhouse Laidback Luke reveals in his recent column on Huffington Post. Apparently listening to fans whinge on social media actually takes its toll, if you choose to listen. In the editorial, the DJ writes that he’s fielded criticisms on Twitter as far afield as followers in Melbourne, Australia (Luke has been championing and experimenting with the ‘Melbourne sound’, what he sees as a “little brother of the Dutch sound”), all the way over to disgruntled fans in Egypt.

“They told me I’m going downhill. That I should be making big tracks like my ‘old’ tracks again,” he writes. For a producer like Laidback Luke, that could mean any number of career stages over the past 20 years, or any one of the “over 3000 tracks” he’s produced in that time.

“To me, my old tracks could be the ones that I produced back in 1995…My Daft Punk style days at the end of the nineties. My electro pop time at around 2003. My big room sound of 2005 and up.”

Rather, his social media detractors listed his relatively recent anthems Turbulence, Timebomb and Natural Disaster as the ‘old’ sound that he’s failing to capture. “In my mind, at this moment, I’m still in that sound and pushing that forward,” he adds. “While a lot of kids are currently putting out tracks that sound like Turbulence meets my Guns On Demo, I’ve already passed that.”

For Laidback Luke, followers sometimes fail to see the bigger picture. “It’s a discussion I have with my older following every two to three years. It seems that when you’re in between 18 and 23 years old, you cling onto certain songs for life… It only sucks for the artist trying to move on,” he claims.

“I see a bigger picture, I’ve always done so in spite of my old followers getting upset with me. I can’t get stuck in the past doing my same sound over and over. That seems very boring to me, it would limit, and kill me, artistically.”

Nonetheless, Laidback Luke insists he doesn’t take the feedback lightly. “I care very much about my scene, our music, my followers and my progression…The only thing I can do is to continue to follow [or lead] my own progress. I’ll be switching on my Ableton now and double check if I can still remember how to work it.”

The Dutchman proved he engages with critique following his Guest Editorial providing his production tips for inthemix. You can read Laidback Luke’s editorial in full on Huffington Post.
