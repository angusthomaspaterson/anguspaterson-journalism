Trent Reznor slams the majors

Angus Paterson · 17 May 2007 · inthemix

While Sydney punters were devastated last week when Trent Reznor cancelled two of his highly anticipated concerts, it appears he’s looking after his fans after all. The Nine Inch Nails frontman has struck out against record labels rorting their customers, slamming his Australian distributor UMG of overcharging fans for copies of the band’s new album Year Zero.

“As the climate grows more and more desperate for record labels, their answer to their mostly self-inflicted wounds seems to be to screw the consumer over even more,” Reznor says on his blog. The primary example given is the “absurd” pricing of Year Zero in music stores, costed at around $35 while the new Avril Lavigne release sells for just over $20 in comparison. “No wonder people steal music,” he says.

“By the way, when I asked a label rep about this his response was: ‘It’s because we know you have a real core audience that will pay whatever it costs when you put something out – you know, true fans. It’s the pop stuff we have to discount to get people to buy.’ So… I guess as a reward for being a ‘true fan’ you get ripped off.”

The other trend he rails at is concept of the ‘maxi’ single, an exercise where multiple versions of the same single are released with each carrying some form of rare B-Side, cover or remix. It entices fans to cough up several times over for the same product and it’s definitely something that Nine Inch Nails have been involved with in the past. Remember seeing several different copies of Closer on the record shelves?

“Nothing but a consumer rip-off that I’ve been talked into my whole career,” he says. “No more. The point is, I am trying my best to make sure the music and items NIN puts in the marketplace have value, substance and are worth you considering purchasing. I am not allowing Capital G (the band’s latest single) to be repackaged into several configurations that result in you getting ripped off.”

Nine Inch Nails fans can expect a full-length remix collection “of substance” to be announced soon.
