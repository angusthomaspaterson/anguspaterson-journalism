Sydney promoter's Underbelly family ties

Angus Paterson · 25 May 2009 · inthemix

Sydney promoter David Freeman, manager of UN Sydney on Oxford Street, the brains behind last year’s Freedom Festival and new owner of Kings Cross nightspot Lady Lux, spoke out to the papers over the weekend about a fact many of his clubbing clientele might not be aware of. David is, in fact, the son of George Freeman, a pivotal figure in Sydney’s organised crime underground from the 1960s to the end of the 1980s, who was this year memorably depicted by actor Peter O’Brien in Underbelly: A Tale of Two Cities.

David revealed to the Sunday Telegraph that while his father passed away nearly 20 years ago, his impact still lives on. “He must have had an impact on people. He’s been dead for 19 years and people still speak about him and regard him highly. He was friends with all the politicians, all the high judges. He gambled with Kerry Packer.”

David (pictured above in a photo taken from his MySpace page)and his brother Adam also shared details of growing up in the environment that’s been depicted in this year’s high-rating season of Underbelly. “All these people would come in to get their wages in envelopes with their name on them. To make us feel special as kids, he’d give us an envelope with our name on it and a few hundred dollars and Smarties and lollies in it.” However, the brothers deny their family has ever been involved in drug dealing. “Dad had nothing to do with drugs. He was just into horses and illegal gambling casinos,” Adam told the paper.

David is looking to step up his involvement in the Sydney clubbing scene shortly. His latest project revolves around popular venue Lady Lux, having entered into a partnership with his father’s old associate John Ibrahim, who worked for George Freeman as a driver and errand boy in his teens but is now involved with around 17 venues in the Kings Cross clubbing district. Ibrahim is a hugely successful entrepreneur who is well known for evading authorities over the years, famously denying allegations he was “the new lifeblood of the drugs trade in Kings Cross” in the Wood Royal Commission into NSW police corruption during the mid 90s.

David, however, vouches for Ibrahim as a legitimate businessman. “He’s buying properties and continues buying new businesses. He doesn’t do anything wrong. He’s completely legit… Police are wasting taxpayers’ money. Give up. They’re never going to get anything on him.” The business partnership involves a $1 million renovation of Lady Lux, and David says that the club will be pursuing a more upmarket focus when it reopens in a few months, offer an expensive wine list, freshly shucked oysters for hungry punters and as well as rooms for hire for $1,500.
