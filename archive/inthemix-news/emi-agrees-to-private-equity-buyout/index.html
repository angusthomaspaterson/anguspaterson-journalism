EMI agrees to private equity buyout

Angus Paterson · 23 May 2007 · inthemix

EMI is selling for a $5.8 billion price tag by the looks of things, as private equity firm Terra Firma pushes aside the other groups tussling with takeover attempts. Some of the other interested parties have included long-standing rival Warner Music and a number of other investment companies, but EMI has picked the Terra Firma bid as its preference.

It’s the sort of private equity negotiations that we’ve seen in Australia with companies like PBL and Qantas; a private equity sale is attractive because it gives a company a big wad of cash to play around with, without them having to contend with any obligations to prying shareholders. And the time is ripe for a shift in ownership for EMI: the company has experienced heavy difficulties in recent times that have been attributed to the explosion of digital music among other things, and the takeover news comes shortly after a reported 37 per cent plunge in full-year earnings.

Although EMI has recommended Terra Firma’s bid, it’s still possible for any of the rivals to clock in with a higher offer. And no one is ruling Warner out of the game just yet – the company has been locked in a takeover battle since 2000 when it first made a play for its music rival and isn’t conceding defeat just yet.
