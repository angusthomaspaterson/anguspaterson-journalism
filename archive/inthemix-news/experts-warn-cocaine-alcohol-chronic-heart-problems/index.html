Experts warn cocaine + alcohol = chronic heart problems

Angus Paterson · 19 November 2009 · inthemix

Drug experts in Britain have warned that young clubbers using cocaine in combination with alcohol run the risk of developing serious liver disease and heart attacks in their 30s, due to the two substances combining to create different toxin cocaethylene. The Observer reported that hospital admissions for cocaine overdoses have doubled in the last five yearsm while death rates amongst ‘fit young people’ have tripled from 4 a week in 1995 to 12 a week today.

“We are seeing more young people using more substances, they don’t see it as a problem, because they are just using at weekends,” treatment expert Karen Colgan told the newspaper. “They do it (drink alcohol) to increase the effects of cocaine, but we have found cases where young people are getting heart palpitations and then going and playing Sunday football,” she added.

A previous US study in 2005 revealed that using cocaine increased users’ heart beat speeds and blood pressure levels, with alcohol enhancing the effect even further. “The drug combination produced a heart rate increase greater than that of cocaine alone,” said scientists from Berkeley. “Cocaine alone increased the subjects’ heart rate by a peak of 12 beats per minute, while cocaine plus ethanol increased heart rate by 33 bpm,” they added.

The issue was also addressed by Scottish drugs expert Tom Wood the same year, who criticised recreational drug users for switching to coke on the basis it was ‘relatively harmless . . . sexy and ‘showbiz’.’

“More idiots in wine bars are shoving it up their noses,” the chairman of the Scottish Association of Alcohol and Drug Action snorted. “They don’t realize that it’s incredibly powerful and dangerous, particularly mixed with alcohol,” he warned.

The drug chief’s clearly ineffective assertions contrasted markedly with views expressed more recently by British pop star Lily Allen who this January suggested the dangers of cocaine are ‘overstated’. “The only story is that drugs are bad and they will kill you – you will become a prostitute, a rapist or a dealer. But that’s not true,” the pop singer pointed out, “I know lots of people that take cocaine three nights a week and get up and go to work everyday, no problem at all,” she added.
