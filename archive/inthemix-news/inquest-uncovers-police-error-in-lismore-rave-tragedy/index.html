Inquest uncovers police error in Lismore rave tragedy

Angus Paterson · 26 May 2009 · inthemix

A coronial inquest this week into the death of Daneeka Nixon at a bush doof event held near Lismore in April 2006 has thrown fresh light on the police investigation surrounding the tragedy. Police have admitted they made an early assumption that the 25-year old’s drowning death was related to drugs and alcohol, which led them to decline to follow up other leads, when a post-mortem examination had in fact uncovered no trace of drugs in her system and only a small amount of alcohol.

AAP reports that Detective Senior Constable Adam Rhodes told the coronial inquest on Monday that police had made an early assumption on the circumstances of Daneeka’s death, based on witness reports she had been seen “roaming around in a state of apparent confusion” before she was last seen heading in the direction of the dam, where her body was retrieved several days later.

“Was an assumption made by police that following a doof party where drugs were consumed … she had wandered off and drowned,” asked Andrew Boe, a representative of Daneeka’s family. “Yes,” replied Detective Rhodes. “And did that close off any desire to investigate whether that assumption was correct?” Mr Boe continued. “There was no injuries or any assault prior to, or any evidence anyone wanted to harm Daneeka,” Rhodes replied.

However, there’s a multitude of questions that remain over police conduct during the investigation. In addition to there not being any drugs detected in her system, police were also aware of a verbal argument that occurred between Daneeka and a male stranger at the party, who has been called to give evidence at the inquest. Detective Rhodes also concedes that it was possible that Daneeka may have been deceased before she entered the dam, as her body was retrieved without her underwear or shoes.

AAP reports that police were also criticised for taking almost a week to find the body, and questions were raised in the inquest over the lack of evidence that any search of the water was conducted (only the perimeter) prior to the discovery of her body. Daneeka’s father Paul had earlier called police to complain about the dam not being searched, but was barred from entering the property after he offered to look himself.

The coroner has confirmed the inquest is expected to continue for the next five days, and ITM will be following the results.
