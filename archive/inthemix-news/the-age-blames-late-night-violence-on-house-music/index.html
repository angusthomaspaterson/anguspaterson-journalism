The Age blames late-night violence on... house music!

Angus Paterson · 8 January 2010 · inthemix

Prepare to have your world rocked. According to Melbourne broadsheet The Age, all of the drunken late-night violence we’ve suffered on our city streets can be blamed on one nasty culprit… house music! “Maybe it’s not the parents. Maybe it’s not the licensing laws,” The Age columnist Fiona Scott-Norman claimed today. “Maybe, just maaaybe, it’s the music played in most of these cattle-pens nightclubs.”

Shock horror! If only we’d know that we’d been wasting all our time listening to “lazy, generic thumping house music.” It’d also explain why so many of us had the urge to randomly glass someone when walking out of a club in the early hours of New Years Day. “I can guarantee you this, Fiona declares. “A man who has danced to the Weather Girls’ It’s Raining Men is not one who leaves the venue and glasses someone in the face. Just sayin’.” How could you possibly argue with that?

Perhaps envisaging the torrent of abuse she’ll inevitably receive from the ITM community (people, start letting us know what you think in the comment field now), Fiona assures us that yes, she admits there’s some OK stuff out there. But most of it is garbage, so there. “All techno, no matter how fabulous, takes the listener on an internal rather than external journey. It’s optimally listened to while under the influence of party drugs manufactured in a bathtub, and ushers you into a self-absorbed dancing trance.”

The Age insists that dance music ultimately encourages self-absorbed behaviour. “It’s all about you. Which is fine if you’re at an eco-rainbow rave, lost in a sea of dreadlocks, but the reason young people head into city centres every weekend, like a river of frisky, spawning salmon nosing their way upstream, is to socialise with other young, frisky people, with an eye to spawning. As it has ever been. And music is as integral a part of the dating and mating ritual as hormones and low self-esteem.”

According to The Age, house music is also to blame for the utter corrosion of our modern dating rituals. It’s dance music’s fault you can’t get a date! “It’s pretty much the ultimate ‘anti-romance’ music. It’s played loud, it’s repetitive, it’s not fun, it’s unremarkable and unmemorable – even if you can make yourself heard over the top, it gives you nothing to talk about, and appears to be the first music ever created by humankind that bypasses the emotions.” And ultimately, all this selfish behaviour and pent-up sexual frustration leads to violence. Damn you house music, look what you’ve done!

But never fear people – we can be rid of all these social problems if we simply got rid of DJs like Danny Tenaglia and Carl Cox, and implemented a change to our music policy. “Playing almost any other kind of music would reduce street violence,” Fiona insists. “Doesn’t matter if it’s disco, funk, yacht rock, indie pop, Mongolian throat-singing, gypsy punk, neo-lounge or Latin, so long as it’s not joyless, thumping background music.”

Say what do you make of The Age’s outlandish claims? ITMers, commence your mocking in the comment field below.
