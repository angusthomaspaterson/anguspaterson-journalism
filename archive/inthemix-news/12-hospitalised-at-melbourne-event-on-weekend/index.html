12 hospitalised at Melbourne event on weekend

Angus Paterson · 22 December 2008 · inthemix

As a timely reminder to punters of the care that needs to be taken during the holiday season, at least 12 drug-affected party-goers were taken to hospital on Saturday evening after attending the X-Qlusive event at Festival Hall in Melbourne, with around another 12 effected punters taken to hospital by their friends. While there were no arrests, Ambulance and first aid staff have pointed to GHB as the source of the hospitalisations.

As reported in Melbourne broadsheet The Age, the drug-takers were stabilised at the venue before being taken to the Royal Melbourne and Alfred hospitals. Senior constable Adam West confirmed that there were no arrests at the party. “Police went to assist ambulance (officers) and did a walk-through of the venue to assist with crowd welfare,” he said. “Our major concern is for the patrons of the venue.”

Health commander for the Metropolitan Ambulance Service Paul Holman has warned punters of the extreme dangers of GHB. “This is not a feelgood drug,” he told ITM. “This is a dengerous drug, and it will poison you. You can party hard without these drugs and still enjoy everything the scene has to offer.” Holman also confirms the promoters did everything they could to cooperate with his staff, and provide a safe event. “I’m not sure what else they could have done in this case, they had all he correct safety procedures in place. I’m not critical at all.”

The hospitalisations have predictably drawn the attention of mainstream media to the dance scene in a context that’s far from positive, and Saturday evening was a timely reminder of the safety concerns that need to be heeded during the summer party season. The stigma of drug overdoses is something that promoters and punters within the dance music community have both tried to move on from, with the hopitalisations on Saturday marring what was otherwise, from all reports, a hugely successful event.

ITM urges the dance community to take care of yourselves and party safe over the New Year period. Check out our Lifestyle Feature for more information on the risks associated with GHB.
