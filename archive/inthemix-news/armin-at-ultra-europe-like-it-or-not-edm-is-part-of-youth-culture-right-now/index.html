Armin at Ultra Europe: “Like it or not, EDM is part of youth culture right now”

Angus Paterson · 14 July 2013 · inthemix

On Saturday night at Ultra Europe, trance don Armin van Buuren followed Porter Robinson’s lead from the previous evening with a set that dominated the mainstage. ITM grabbed Armin shortly before he began to glean a few insights about playing alongside the new generation of EDM high-rollers; tellingly, he was set to hit the stage immediately after Afrojack.

“It makes it very interesting,” he laughed. “But I don’t see it as a challenge, I see it as fun. They book Armin van Buuren, so they get Armin van Buuren. Afrojack, Avicii, Hardwell, these guys are doing amazingly well. But it’s not the kind of music that I think I should be playing.”

Of trance’s original superstars, Dutch veteran Tiesto has maintained his position as a mainstage headliner via a carefully-managed evolution of his sound. Armin is the exception, though: an avowed trance believer headlining Ultra Europe, as well the Miami event that inspired it.

“The attention spans on a lot of dancefloors is a lot shorter than it used to be,” he told inthemix. “You can do two things. You can be stale and do your own thing, and just play a full two-hour set of instrumental trance. Or you can go with the crowd a little, make a bridge and try to keep the Afrojack audience. Try to persuade them to get them into trance…Every gig, I see how far I can stretch it. I usually end up 138-BPM full-blown trance in my sets anyway.”

Armin has played his fair share of large-scale events in Europe during his career, though Ultra in Croatia has proved a different kind of experience, transplanting the American EDM revolution to a new continent.

“Whether you like it or not, EDM is part of youth culture right now,” Armin mused to inthemix. “Like punk used to be, and like The Beatles used to be in the ‘60s. EDM is the Beatles music of 2013.” Stay tuned for the full report from Night Two of Ultra Europe.
