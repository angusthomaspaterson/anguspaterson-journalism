Hyperlinks now a target as MP3 war rages on

Angus Paterson · 21 December 2006 · inthemix

If they needed any more excuse to push ahead with their battle against the MP3 revolution, the ever-vigilant record industry has now been given the go ahead to pursue and prosecute individuals who link to copyrighted material from websites, blogs or MySpace pages. A ruling handed down by the Federal Court upheld an earlier decision that Stephen Cooper, the operator of mp3s4free.net, along with the ISP that hosts the website, were both guilty of infringing copyright laws because they provided a search engine that linked through to illegal MP3 files.

While mp3s4free.net argued that it was in the clear because it did not directly host any copyright-protected music, the Federal Court insisted that it had infringed copyright laws simply by linking through to the illegal material.

“Mr Cooper had power to prevent the communication of copyright sound recordings to the public in Australia via his website,” the judges claimed in the ruling. “He had that power because he was responsible for creating and maintaining his mp3s4free website.”

The court found that E-Talk, the ISP that hosts the mp3s4free website, profited from the copyright infringement through advertisements on the site and took no efforts to take the service down. “E-Talk countenanced the infringing downloading by internet users who visited the website that it hosted,” the court claimed. “The fact is that E-Talk could have prevented the infringements that actually occurred.”

The case against Cooper was brought by 36 parties including the big boys in the recording industry like BMG, EMI, Festival Records, Universal Music and Warner Music. A copy of the ruling can be read HERE.

While it’s unclear how the ruling will impact everyday net users, it represents a win for the record companies as the war against illegal downloading rages on.
