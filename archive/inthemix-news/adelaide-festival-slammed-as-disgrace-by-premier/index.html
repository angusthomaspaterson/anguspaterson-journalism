Adelaide festival slammed as "disgrace" by Premier

Angus Paterson · 14 April 2009 · inthemix

A brand new festival just outside of Adelaide over the weekend called Live At The Zoo has made a rocky entrance into the crowded festival market, with the three-day camping event branded as chaotic, widely disorganised, suffering the cancellation of Saturday night’s headliner, with the organisers even copping it from South Australian Premier Mike Rann via Twitter of all places. “Live at Zoo music festival a disgrace. Chimps could have done a better job than private promoters. Shame because Zoo terrific.”

Live at the Zoo was billed as Adelaide’s very first 3-night, 2-day music and arts festival, with a lineup that boasted local acts like Cut Copy, Bliss N Eso, Van She, Grafton Primary among plenty of other popular local acts. The location was at a 200 acre plain at Monarto Zoo just outside of Adelaide, but while it sounded idyllic, the bad news had already started to filter out by Friday afternoon. More than 300 cars were reportedly banked up outside the festival at one stage (see photo below), with punters kept waiting for hours as security guards checked every vehicle for alcohol and drugs. Local rag the Adelaide Advertiser published allegations on Friday that festival staff had quit on the spot when they realised the level of disorganization at the event.

The main complaints centred around a lack of toilets, with the remaining ones blocked and overflowing for much of the three days, as well as a distinct lack of running water, showers and the camping areas. The festival reached its lowest point on Saturday night when headliner Cut Copy’s performance was cancelled. ABC News reports that security fencing had to be reinforced after the crowd surged forward through the makeshift barriers, with the delay meaning the band would have played past the midnight curfew if they’d been allowed to hit the stage.

The event received the harshest of all rundowns in a special report in the Adelaide Advertiser on Tuesday. “Billed as South Australia’s first three-day camping festival, sadly it could very well be our last,” wrote Sam Kelton. “When music lovers handed over $185 to ‘be an animal for a weekend’, many were unaware they would actually have to live like one.”
