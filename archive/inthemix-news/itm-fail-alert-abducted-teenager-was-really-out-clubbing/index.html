ITM Fail Alert: 'Abducted' teenager was really out clubbing

Angus Paterson · 7 September 2009 · inthemix

We’ve all told a sneaky lie to our parents at some point, to cover up our nefarious deeds and dealings that we wanted to keep under wraps. Perhaps you told your mother that you having a sleepover at a mate’s house, when you were really slamming vodka jelly shots and blowing up letterboxes at an unsupervised house party? Classic. Well, in the case of 18 year old Northern Territory girl Jessica, she told her mother she had been abducted from outside the front of her house at 3AM this past Sunday morning – when she was really out clubbing at Darwin’s Discovery Nightclub.

The key to effectively lying to your parents is to keep it simple, and we’ve gotta admit, Jessica fell down a little in this regard. She told her mother they’d driven around for 10 minutes, before she fought off her attackers and escaped into a nearby park – where her memory conveniently blanked out for a around 10 hours, with her next recollection waking up at a bus stop at 1PM the next day. Right…

After Jessica called her mother and told her about the abduction, she was taken to the Casuarina Police Station to report the incident, sending Darwin police on a wild goose chase for several hours looking for the imaginary culprits. Of course, it wasn’t too long before Jessica was caught out.

The Northern Territory News took predictable relish in rubbing Jessica’s face in her indiscretions, extracting an apology out of her before publically naming and shaming her in the paper (complete with a sad-faced photograph). “I know what I’ve done is wrong – I’m sorry for making up the story and wasting police time.”

Jessica’s mother agreed with the Northern Territory News that public humiliation through the local media was the most constructive way to deal with Jessica’s misbehavior. “Her punishment will now be that she has to live with what she has done to the police, me and everyone else who was concerned for her,” she said.

Take that Jessica! That’ll teach you to think twice about lying to Mum (or at least to put a little more thought into it next time, you amateur).
