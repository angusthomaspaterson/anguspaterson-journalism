Playground Weekender wraps up - check out photos on ITM

Angus Paterson · 12 March 2008 · inthemix

While tens of thousands of people were at Future Music Festival over the weekend, there was another essential event going down out at Wisemans Ferry. Regarded by many as the informed music lover’s choice when stacked up against all the other stuff going on over the weekend, the numbers might have been lower than the other big ticket events but in contrast, the response from attendees was ecstatic, with more than a few people whispering that it was the “best festival ever.” Check out the photos on ITM as well as the definitive review from ITM’s own Jack Tregonning.

Check out the photos here.
Check out the ITM review here.
And for a bit of extra rock-centric coverage, have a peek at Fasterlouder.

One of the most unique things about the event is that it seeks to emulate the vibe created at some of Europe’s best festivals like Glastonbury, by spreading the festival over three days. “Too often festivals are a fleeting exchange. You’re herded in, watch some bands, scuff the grass, dirty the portaloos and leave behind a trail of beer cans. Not so with Playground Weekender, a festival that’s more slowburn romance than throwaway fling,” as it’s said in the ITM review.

And with a fine selection of electronic artists on hand including Steve Bug, Tiefscwartz, Ben Watt, LTJ Bukem and Kruder & Dorfmeister*, alongside a fine selection of indie/rock artists like The Wombats, Ian Brown and Maximo Park, the musical output was somehow able to match the beauty of the natural surroundings and the party kept going well into Sunday evening. The numbers thin out with each arriving ferry, but you can tell who’s in it for the long haul. It’s hard to convey how unlike any other festival Playground is, especially on a balmy Sunday when all the colours, from the river to the hills behind, are so vivid. Everyone who disembarks at Del Rio seems to understand what the three days are for.”

Just check out the forums for an idea of how well received the three-day event was. “Till next time Playground. No other festival comes close.”
