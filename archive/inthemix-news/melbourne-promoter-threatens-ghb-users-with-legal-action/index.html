Melbourne promoter threatens GHB users with legal action

Angus Paterson · 7 August 2009 · inthemix

“If anybody comes to my event, takes GHB, overdoses, then needs medical treatment and resources, I will sue them. I will sue because it tarnishes my reputation and it ruins my business.’’ This week Richie McNeill, head of Melbourne promoter Hardware and one of the driving forces behind this week’s massive Stereosonic announcement, took the next step against GHB users by telling the mainstream press he will sue punters who overdose at his events.

‘‘There are no deterrents for young people who dabble in GHB,’’ McNeill told the Herald Sun, claiming a tougher stance is needed to soften the impact on the dance scene. GHB overdoses have already cost Melbourne the Defqon.1 Festival this year, with ID&T’s Duncan Stutterheim claiming it impacted his company’s decision not to bring Trance Energy back in 2010.

Following the article, which appeared in the Herald Sun earlier this week, Richie McNeill continued the debate in the ITM forums. “So if someone is going to come into our event, and show no respect for themselves, for us, for the performers, for the fellow event punters, and they assault someone, or blow-out on GHB or alcohol and attract negative damaging feedback in the media to our event (which can and does inherent your ability to find venues and do business), then we feel if it does cost us financially in some capacity on a big enough scale, we will sue that person for damages.”

While McNeill admits it may be a long shot in the legal sense, “at least we have started something which hopefully other venues, promoters etc… will think about themselves implementing. A zero tolerance policy in this sense on this behaviour.”
