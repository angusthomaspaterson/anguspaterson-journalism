Starbucks moves into music

Angus Paterson · 14 March 2007 · inthemix

Would you like some of the latest underground tunes with your frappuccino? Not such an unlikely scenario with the news that global caffeine dealer Starbucks is continuing on its quest to take over the world with it forming its own record label. Part of the multinational’s push by the company to expand the entertainment side of its operations, the enterprise will be called Hear Music which is the label already affiliated with the compilation CDs sold in Starbucks coffee houses since 1999.

Starbucks describes the venture as a source of “new and convenient ways for you to discover, experience and acquire great music.” It’ll be partnering with Concord Music Group for the venture and it seems like a pretty tight relationship so far – the two have previously worked together on a Ray Charles compilation that sold in excess of 5 million copies.

There’s been some murmuring from the record industry about the move, with people both cynical that Starbucks actually have what it takes to run a record label as well as worried that such a massive franchise has enough global weight to throw around to actually bring about another paradigm shift in the way music is sold. Out with the iTunes and in with the coffee houses? And what sort of credible music will Starbucks really bring to the table anyway?

In response to these questions, the company has issued a statement claiming that with the new venture it will “advocate creative control for artists and encourages musicians to stretch and take risks.”
