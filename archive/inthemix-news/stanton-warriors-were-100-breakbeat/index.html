Stanton Warriors - "We're 100% breakbeat"

Angus Paterson · 29 December 2008 · inthemix

“We try to steer away from being called breakbeat artists, because it’s seen as a bit of a dirty word.” The Stanton Warriors ignited a firestorm last month when they uttered these words to online music mag www.noiseporn.com, stirring up rage amongst the tight-knit breakbeat scene when ironically, the UK DJ/producer duo had only just released the finest breaks release of the year in the form of Stanton Sessions vol. 3 (ranking extremely high in ITM’s Top 20 Releases of 2008!). ITM recently spoke to Dominic Butler from the Stanton Warriors ahead of their tour with the Good Vibrations festival in February, and he was intent on setting the record straight on the duo’s ties to the breaks scene.

“We’re definitely not anti-breaks,” Dom told ITM. “In the world of big breaks DJs, so to speak, we’re actually one of the only ones who’re not playing 4/4 or dubstep or whatever, so it’s quite ironic.” The misunderstanding stemmed from Dom mentioning in the interview that on their recent US tour, their sound had been referred to as ‘disco bass’ by the punters. “I’d said they can call it whatever they want, that’s fine by me. That got misconstrued as me saying that Stanton Warriors had changed our sound.”

The outraged reaction from breaks purists had stemmed from the fact that with the seminal releases so few and far between in 2008, a perceived denial and distancing from the scene by the Stanton Warriors came as a particularly heavy blow. But Dominic insists you only need to have a quick listen to their live sets, or Stanton Sessions vol. 3 itself, to have the issue clarified. “There’s fundamentally a broken beat throughout the whole album, and I think that’s a testament to the fact that we haven’t moved away or turned our back on breaks,” he told ITM. “We are flying the flag. We haven’t given in, we’re not playing 30% house in our sets or token dubstep tunes. We’re sticking with our formula of broken beats 100%.”

“When all is said and done, we’ve put a lot of years, time and effort into the sound. We’re in it for life, and we definitely want to see the whole thing move forward.”

Have a listen to the Stanton’s recent mix in ITM-FM, and stay tuned for the full-length feature interview as the Good Vibrations tour draws close in early 2009.
