Ultra Records' Patrick Moxey at IMS: "There is no bubble to burst"

Angus Paterson · 23 May 2013 · inthemix

“We’ve worked so hard to get the scene through the barriers, now we have all the resources at our fingertips that we could ever want. I can only feel excited that the larger world has finally embraced what we’ve been trying to do for so many years.”

IMS Ibiza kicked off in the rather opulent surrounds of the Ibiza Gran Hotel on Wednesday morning, with a large contingent of industry players flying in from all around the world, timed with a posse of high-profile opening parties kicking off over the next few days.

Conference co-founder Pete Tong was on hand to lend his unmistakable presence to the proceedings, introducing the first keynote speaker Patrick Moxey; head of Ultra Records and since the beginning of this year, President of Electronic Music at Sony Records. The Ultra Records stable includes heavyweight names like Kaskade, Tiesto, Above & Beyond, Ferry Corsten and Deadmau5. Its scope also covers everything from hard dance headliner Headhunterz to wonky beatmaker TOKiMONSTA.

Flying high on the worldwide success of the new Daft Punk record on Sony, Moxey had largely positive things to say about the future, insisting the long-term experience of the players who’ve remained in the industry, and the ‘DIY’ culture of dance music, is what will ensure its longevity.

“We started with Ultra Records in 1995, and at that time dance was pretty much a dirty word,” Moxey told the audience. “It took a lot of time, a lot of perseverance, and a lot of people – the DJs, the booking agents, the labels like ourselves – all working hard to change people’s attitudes towards electronic music. We had to create our own infrastructure, and over the years it really became a bunch of like-minded, independent people working together with a real DIY attitude and mentality. That is what has built our scene and our movement as strong as it is today. There is no bubble to burst; everything you see is very much organic.”

Moxey emphasised the strong foundations of a scene that existed for so long outside of the establishment. “We’re going to take advantage of all the things we didn’t have before the boom,” he told the audience. “With Ultra, we’d already arrived at a place, completely independently, where we had licensees in many territories across the world. So when the opportunity came up with Sony being interested in investing with Ultra, in my mind it was the ultimate alliance. They’ve got 4,000 people on our side who are used to selling music on a daily basis. The resources we have now to help our artists and our music are massive.

“I’m very pleased with how things are going. Because really, you can’t turn the clock back. Everyone always seek purity and authenticity, and that’s great, because that really drives how we got here in the first place. However, you’ll still always continue to see though the subcultures within the larger culture of dance music. For me it’s very encouraging that beyond the boom, there is something for everyone.”
