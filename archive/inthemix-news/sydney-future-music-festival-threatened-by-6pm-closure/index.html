Sydney Future Music Festival threatened by 6pm closure

Angus Paterson · 25 November 2008 · inthemix

UPDATE – The inthemix community petition to save the 2009 Future Music Festival in Sydney is now closed, if you’d like to read the letter submitted with the list of names just head here!

One of ITM’s favourite festivals is scheduled to return to Sydney’s Randwick Racecourse early next year, but there’s been a spanner thrown in the works which could threaten the chances of it returning to the venue. It’s been recommended that the finish time for Future Music Festival be moved forward from 10pm to 6pm in 2009, and Randwick councilors will be voting on the recommendations tonight.

If the councilors vote ‘yes’ on the recommendations, promoters Future Entertainment have claimed we may not see FMF happen at Randwick Racecourse next year at all, it’s been reported in today’s Sydney Morning Herald. “We wouldn’t be able to bring large international acts to Australia to perform at these festivals,” said Brett Robinson from Future. “A number of them will be ruled out purely because they can’t perform after hours. So if it was a 6pm close at Randwick, that would be very difficult for them.”

He’s of course referring to acts like last year’s headliners the Chemical Brothers, who brought with them a spectacular visual setup – the sort of spectacle that would be rendered completely pointless if it was playing to thousands of punters during broad daylight at 5pm.

Local police have supported the council’s request for a 6pm close, and they’ve also rebuffed Future Entertainment’s request to increase capacity from 35,000 to 50,0000 in 2009. In addition, they’ve called for a limit of two alcoholic drinks per customer at each sale, and for liquor not to be served after 5pm. “This is in the community interest and will assist with noise, litter and the movement of patrons for the duration of the event,” a police spokesman said.

However, Future Entertainment insist that the proper measures are in place for next year to ensure an event that rolls out smoothly, pointing to the ticket price including free public transport for punters as an example. “Our planning for these events [is] very extensive and our primary concern is to do with the health and safety of our staff and patrons and to minimise the impact on [neighbours],” he said.
