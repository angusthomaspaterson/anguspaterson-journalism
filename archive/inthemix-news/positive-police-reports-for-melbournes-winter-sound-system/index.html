Positive police reports for Melbourne's Winter Sound System

Angus Paterson · 8 June 2009 · inthemix

While the mainstream media was filled with news on the 65 arrests that occurred at Melbourne’s Winter Sound System festival over the weekend, Victoria Police have returned largely positive feedback on the event, praising the professionalism of promoters Future Entertainment and claiming that for an event that hosted 17,000 people, the number of drug arrests was relatively low.

Police Superintendent Trevor Parks told ABC News that there were no reports of assaults, and claimed the event was well organised. “I actually think that’s a good reflection on the quality and the quantity of security, and the way the promoter runs the event, very professional,” he said.

Parks also said the number of drug related arrests were relatively low. “We’ve had a crowd at Myer Music Bowl recently of I think 7,000 people – we had 83 drug-related matters, so that’s a significant number when you compare the crowd to the numbers charged.”

Police also defended to the Herald Sun their “lenient” approach to the drug arrests, which saw all but three of the 65 arrested punters receiving a caution as opposed to being pushed through the courts and slapped with a criminal record. “We’re unhappy people are arrested and take drugs at these particular activities, but it’s a fact of life. We don’t see it as a soft-handed approach. The trafficking matters are more serious and they will come before the courts,” Parks told the Herald Sun.

“We try and perhaps steer people away from drugs in the first instance because we really don’t want your son or daughter going to court for having a couple of pills. There are other alternatives to deal with it.”
