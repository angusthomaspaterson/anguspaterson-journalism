Ecstasy use spreads outside Australian nightclubs

Angus Paterson · 26 October 2007 · inthemix

If it wasn’t something we were already well aware of, a new report released at a national drug conference on the Gold Coast has shown that ecstasy use has moved well beyond the clubbing scene. More than one million Australians have tried it, making it our country’s most popular illegal drug after marijuana.

Greg Fowler, a senior researcher at the University of Queensland, has claimed that around 1.2 million Australians over the age of 14 had tried ecstasy, reported in The Australian. More than 550,000 people had tried the drug in 2004, and usage levels are also rising dramatically with the proportion of Australians who have tried ecstasy shooting up from 3.1 per cent in 1993 to 7.5 per cent in 2004.

And, if there‘s was still any perception out there that ecstasy use was restricted just to nightclubs and dance parties, the research is telling of something different entirely. “The way that people are using ecstasy has changed,” says Fowler, with his study indicating that ecstasy users tend to be young, white, well-educated and middle class. “Based on these [sic] data, Australia is believed to be one of the highest per capita ecstasy consuming countries in the world,” the report says.

inthemix spoke to Paul Dillon from Drug & Alcohol Research Australia, and he said that usage trends in Australia are bucking the figures seen in the rest of the world. “If you go to Europe you’ll see that ecstasy use is on the decline, while in the States the use is stable and declining slightly. But here we continue to rise; we’ve almost quadrupled in the past decade in terms of use, and that’s definitely a significant increase.”

The reason behind this increase? The main factor, according to Dillon, is the throttled supply of cocaine to Australia. “In Europe cocaine is a major issue. The quality of cocaine is high, the price is relatively cheap and as a result, there’s a range of drugs to use over there. Here we don’t have that, so ecstasy has maintained its market share.”

He also supports the assertion that ecstasy use has moved beyond the nightclubs. “I don’t think that’s anything new,” he says. “There’s a range of different ways and situations in which people use the drug, of course it remains very closely linked to the dance scene… But there are many other groups who use the drug as well.”
