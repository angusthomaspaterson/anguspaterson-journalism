Paul Oakenfold headlines Sydney's Mardi Gras

Angus Paterson · 14 January 2009 · inthemix

That ol’ hand of the global clubbing scene, and arguably one of the figures responsible for pushing dance culture out into the mainstream, Paul Oakenfold will of course be back in the country for the first time in several years to play Future Music Festival across the country. That’s not all though, as he’s been also been announced as the headliner for the Australian gay scene’s night of nights, the party that accompanies Sydney’s annual Mardi Gras parade.

“I’ve always wanted to play at Mardi Gras Sydney,” Oakenfold told ITM on the phone this week. “It’ll be an honour to do it. From the day I came to Sydney, I was told that the biggest and best party is Mardi Gras. So I’m like, bring it on! I never got the opportunity because of previous commitments. But they’ve been very nice to me and kept me on their radar and now I can come and do it.”

Oakie says he’s well aware of the heritage of both the party and the Mardi Gras parade itself, and how tightly it’s been tied in with the development of gay and lesbian culture in Australia over the past three decades, and putting gay rights on the agenda for public discussion. Now based in the US, with a weekly residency in Las Vegas at Rain Nightclub every Saturday night, Oakenfold says he’s been tuned into his adopted home’s recent misdemeanors as far as gay rights goes.

“We’ve just had big demonstrations in California because there was a right for gay and lesbian marriage, and they took that right away,” Oakenfold told ITM. “I think it’s silly in this day and age that you’re judged by your sexual preference.” He sees playing at an event like Mardi Gras as a way of demonstrating his support for the cause.
