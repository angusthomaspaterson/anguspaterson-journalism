Drug dogs descend on the Gold Coast

Angus Paterson · 11 May 2009 · inthemix

The clubs, festivals and public spaces of the Gold Coast are the latest to be targeted in sniffer dog operations, with police descending on Broadbeach Mall on Saturday night for ‘Operation Pounce’. 24 officers were involved and 28 revelers searched, but only four people were arrested on drug-related charges.

However, News Limited local rag The Bulletin could barely contain its glee over the Gold Coast getting a visit from our four-legged friends. “You have been warned. Police have stepped up the war on drugs on the Gold Coast and have promised they will be coming to a nightclub near you – but you won’t know when,” The Bulletin said today. ‘Operation Pounce’ was labeled a “success” by the paper (who also published an online video of a punter being punched by a police office when he patted one of the dogs), and Queensland police say that it’s a sign of things to come.

“It’s something you’re going to see more of … suddenly and without warning,” Broadbeach Sergeant Shane Holmes told The Bulletin. “We’re fortunate enough that the legislation exists for us to bring the drug-detection dogs into public places and use them as another weapon in our arsenal against drug offenders and anti-social behaviour. Certainly the element of surprise is an important factor. This type of operation is just one of many strategies that we will be employing in our continued targeting of drug use in licensed venue precincts.”

‘Operation Pounce’ accounts for the first time that police have targeted patrons who are either walking the streets, or waiting to enter nightclubs. “While we don’t have specific intelligence telling us drug use is particularly rife around the licensed venues in the precinct, there is a connection we have made between drugs and the anti-social behaviour we sometimes see. Just based on the law of probabilities, we were likely to come up with some positive detections.”
