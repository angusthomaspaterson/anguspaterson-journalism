Middle-aged rave revival that's resurrecting retired clubbers

Angus Paterson · 14 April 2009 · inthemix

A ‘middle-aged club revival’ driven by ‘clubbers who raved hard in the 1990s’ is sweeping across the UK. So says former Mixmag’ editor (and ex hard raver) Dom Philips, in an article he wrote for the UK’s Sunday Times. He points to the packed touring schedule of veteran DJs like Jeremy Healy and John Kelly as evidence, though he suggests that most of the previously open-minded clubbers had become conservative in both their dress sense and musical taste.

“Many feel that the “superclub” explosion of the mid to late 1990s sucked the heart and soul out of their scene,” Philips explained, “So they prefer the idealistic house music of the late 1980s and early 1990.”

The feature appeared over a decade after the Independent first identified the same group of ‘over 30 but won’t grow up’ ravers, calling them ‘Mr and Ms Middle Youth.’ “They are Britain’s middle children: for whom Ecstasy- influenced dance culture is the defining social characteristic,” the Independent described in 1997. “She goes weekend clubbing where she and her friends occasionally indulge in Class A drugs, although she’s usually home in time for a few hours’ sleep before a visit to the garden centre,” the paper described. “He is also in his thirties, a Nick Hornby-esque middle man, straddling the divide between Loaded laddery and DIY maturity, a lager-damaged liver and prostate trouble,” said the paper.

In other middle news, a raving mad Welsh mother of five was last week locked up after appearing in court for abandoning her children, after telling them she was ‘off to Ibiza’. The unnamed Mum flew to Malaga for five days last September, explaining at Merthyr Crown Court that she’d become exasperated by her children’s incessant nagging. “I wanted to teach them a lesson, they are always moaning,” she complained, “Everything got on top of me. I just wanted to go.” (WalesOnline.co.uk)

She was later sentenced to six months in jail, suspended after explaining she’d been working 15 hours straight, on two jobs. ‘I’m going to spend however long it takes making it up to my children,” she told reporters, “I hope eventually they’ll forgive me.’ (Daily Mail)
