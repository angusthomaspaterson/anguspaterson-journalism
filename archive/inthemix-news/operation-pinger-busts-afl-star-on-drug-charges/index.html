'Operation Pinger' busts AFL star on drug charges

Angus Paterson · 7 August 2009 · inthemix

Prepare to sit back, shake your head and be amazed by the creativity of the Victorian Police Force. This week Essendon footballer Nathan Lovett-Murray was charged with drug possession as part of an eight-month police investigation – codenamed ‘Operation Pinger’, no less. The result of the raid on Lovett-Murray’s premises? Police seized a single ecstasy pill (or a ‘pinger’, as the case may be).

Police have confirmed that Lovett-Murray faces charges, with the raid on his home one of the 11 that was conducted across Melbourne and regional Victoria that saw another five men charged.

AFL portal realfooty.com.au reports that Sergeant Paul Majda claimed police had “no alternative but to charge him [Lovett-Murray] with being in possession of a drug of dependence”.

Before falling into the cataclysmic crossfire of ‘Operation Pinger’, Nathan Lovett-Murray’s reputation was otherwise untarnished, and the athlete was regarded as one of the Bombers’ most respected players, known for his strong support of indigenous issues. Essendon managing director Peter Jackson told The Age this week that Lovett-Murray would be supported because, “we know his character, we know what he does in the community, we know what he does for his family, friends and his people.”
