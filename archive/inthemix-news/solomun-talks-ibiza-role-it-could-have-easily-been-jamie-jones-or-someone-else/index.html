Solomun talks Ibiza role: "It could have easily been Jamie Jones or someone else"

Angus Paterson · 28 May 2013 · inthemix

It’s been a big week for Solomun on the White Isle. Arriving in Ibiza to play Friday’s IMS Grand Finale Festival at the spectacular Dalt Villa alongside Sven Vath, Scuba and Maya Jane Coles, he also stepped up as a mentor during the Burn Residency program that took place during the IMS conference.

Further still, Sunday night saw the opening party for his Solomun +1 residency at Pacha, a pivotal component of the club’s new strategy that’s seen it shed the likes of Tiesto and Erick Morillo in favour of a distinctly ‘underground’-led line-up.

“The thing is, you do what you do,” Solomun told inthemix, referring to both his own profile, which soared after a gang-busters 2012 season in Ibiza, as well as that of his Diynamic Music label. “In the end, we haven’t really changed anything. Though the label is always growing, and you sometimes go in new directions, it’s something we’ve always felt positive about.”

He attributes the growing interest in his label’s style of music as a general shift towards deeper house and techno in dance music. “A lot of DJs have stripped their music a little bit down, and a lot of artists have gone in this direction. And of course, [there was] Ibiza last year. I won the award for ‘Best Producer’ award, and for them that is a ‘sign’. I am now the face in Ibiza for this movement. But it could have easily been Jamie Jones or someone else.”

During the IMS conference, Solomun was involved in mentoring the cast of 18 aspiring DJs who were gathered in Ibiza for the Burn Residency program, trained by an elite cast of talent that also included Steve Lawler, Maceo Plex and the always-irreverent Fatboy Slim.

As well as offering practical production advice for a selection of the DJs’ tracks he’d hand-picked himself, he stressed the benefits of being aligned with labels with strong pedigrees like Diynamic Music, as a means of getting noticed in the extremely crowded digital era.

“We actually have a good network in terms of being able to take talent out to people, and a power to promote the music, and then besides, we always try to take care of our artists. Besides all this DJing stuff, I am always there for my artists, and they can always call me.”

Solomun’s own aspiring artist that he has got behind is the young David August, a major priority for Diynamic this year after the label released his debut Times album. “I always tell my artists that Diynamic is a flagship; you can choose it, you’re given a good base, and then it’s in your hands. Besides that, what’s also true is that you need a little bit of luck, or a lot of luck, to get noticed.”
