Mark Dynamix & John Course - The Annual 2004

Angus Paterson · 19 November 2003 · inthemix

(Ministry of Sound)

As we fast approach the end of the year, we also move toward the impending release of a number of dance compilations that commemorate the year that was 2003. With Kid Kenobi’s excellent Breaks 03 having already provided a source of the biggest and best breaks tracks of the year, now comes the release of the Ministry of Sound 2004 Annual. Having provided a much more credible alternative to their cheesy UK counterparts in the past, the Aussie Annuals have managed to stick to the MOS formula but at the same time retain a high level of musical quality. Veteran Sydney hero Mark Dynamix was a foregone conclusion to provide the duties in regards to tech, electro, trance and everything in between, while Melbourne’s John Course has this time been chosen to represent the funky house side of things.

With a legacy in the Sydney dance scene that goes way back even before his days as a Sublime resident at the old Pitt Street venue, Mark Dynamix is all class regardless of what genre he is playing in. And for this year’s MOS Annual mix, Dynamix dishes up a tasty serving of tech, electro and house, with a dash of breaks on the side. Beginning with one of the year’s most instantly recognisable tracks in the form of Benny Benassi’s Satisfaction, almost immediately he ups the pace with Ferry Corsten’s heavily electro-fied trance anthem Rock Your Body, Rock, a tune that will no doubt provide Ferry with another mega hit as it rides in on the electro-clash wave.

As a matter of fact, the CD is absolutely choc-full of electro: Ewan Pearson’s mix of Goldfrapp’s Strict Machine remains as heavily electroclash as the original; Snap’s The Power is a evil tech-stomper with a bassline that merges perfectly into Rogue Trader’s absolutely inspired mix of INXS classic One of My Kind. The tracks come thick and fast with over 20 included on the CD, and Dynamix winds them in and then out again in the blink of an eye. There are just too many good tunes to mention in the limited space of a review, but among the most memorable include an offering from the Boogie Pimps. Constructed around a sample from the Jefferson Airplane classic Somebody to Love, the track sounds absolutely superb, and the mix is wound up not much later with Cam Farrar’s Wasted, another track that has insidiously entered all of our consciousnesses over the last year.

I came to two conclusions after one particularly rocking listening session in my bedroom, the first of which is that the CD is probably the most technically proficient put together by Dynamix yet. With an incredible ear for what sounds good in a mix and how to pace the tracks, his CD is so superbly executed that it almost sounds organic. The second conclusion was that it sounds almost nothing like a typical MOS Annual CD. Sure, the album features more than a few tracks that have been recognisably present over the last 12 months, but many of them are far from “anthems,” and Dynamix’s approach seems to be to choose obscure remixes over the original tracks (demonstrated in his decision to include Eric Prydz’s rendition of Futureshock’s Pride’s Paranoia for example). Dynamix demonstrates his talent for eclecticism as he effortlessly moves through the genres, but punters who aren’t such a fan of tech and electro may feel a little left out in the cold. In his strive to mix an awesome CD, Dynamix compromised somewhat on the Annual formula; whether that’s a good thing or a bad thing is up to you.

The director of the Vicious record label (and just as much of a legend in his hometown of Melbourne as Dynamix is in Sydney), Jon Course’s CD represents the funky house side of the spectrum of 2003. The upside of choosing a more credible name like Course is that he tends to steer well clear of the cheese, while managing to stay well within the boundaries of an accessible mix CD. Starting off the proceedings with the wonderfully funky Make Luv by Room 5, Course follows it up with Junior Jack’s E Samba, a percussion-filled house anthem with a bassline more than a little similar to the one found in Thrill Me.

Course’s Annual CD is full of tracks that were absolutely huge during 2003, including DJ Sneak’s Kitchen Sink, E-Funk’s Shout, and Double D’s Shining, an uplifting house anthem that is accessible yet not enough to grate on your nerves. Etherfox’s The Whirled You Live segues beautifully into Groove Armada’s Final Shakedown, and the Jason Nevins interpretation of the Michael Jackson classic Human Nature is a welcome addition to the mix. Overall, Course delivers an Annual offering that is a more than competent look at the year that has just gone. The irony however, is that by sticking more closely to the Annual formula, Jon Course’s mix is not nearly as inspiring as Dynamix’s CD.

The Australian Ministry of Sound crew have once again demonstrated their ability to pick the cream of the crop in terms of our nation's DJ talent, and have delivered yet another quality release. The distinct lack of “anthems” on Dynamix’s CD is a move that will blissfully win some over and alienate others, while on the other side of the coin, many will slip comfortably in to Jon Course’s CD while others may feel a little bored. But in the end this review barely even matters, as it won’t stop thousands of clubbers around the nation from rushing out to buy it – because once again, the Ministry of Sound 2004 Annual remains an essential purchase.
