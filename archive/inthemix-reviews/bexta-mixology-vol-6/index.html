Bexta - Mixology Vol. 6

Angus Paterson · 18 April 2004 · inthemix

(Central Station Records)

Bexta has earned herself a reputation for being the Jerry Bruckheimer of DJs: all flashy style and no substance. Whether you are a fan or not, a strong case can be argued that her success as a DJ has been a little less consistent than as a producer. While she has a producer’s ear for what bombs to drop on the dance floor, her mixing and pacing often leaves a little to be desired. Nonetheless, her Mixology series of mix CDs have been big sellers, and the latest Mixology Vol. 6 has recently been unveiled to the public at large.

As has been the tradition with her Mixology CDs in the past, the first CD is not quite as hard as the music she is normally associated with, the focus more on tough house and uplifting trance. If the mixing isn’t great, then some of the tunes certainly are, with the CD featuring some nice and fresh sounding trancey tunes. The Paul Oakenfold mix of Born Slippy sounds great, John ‘00’ Flemming’s Le Voyage provides some dreamy uplifting trance, while M-Shape’s Cloudless Sky showcases some harder edged trancey sounds. The second CD is more familiar territory for fans, with many of the tracks contain that bangin’, relentless energy that Bexta is known for. She shows why she made a name for herself with the pumping trance of Johnny Rock’s Feel the Pain, with other quality selections including DJ TT Hacky’s I Want You and Sound 2 Light’s It’s Like That.

But while many of the tunes may rock individually, as a whole the whole CD is, well, pretty bland. While her beat matching is competent enough, Bexta serves up some pretty pedestrian mixing by never bothering to mix in key, with very few memorable hooks to pull you in and keep you there. The reason for this is quite elusive and hard to put your finger on, as a lot of the individual tracks are really quite good and further prove that Bexta has an ear for what sounds good in a club. But in the end it just hammers home the point that DJing is about so much more than mixing two quality records together: to quote a cliché, it’s about taking the listener on a “journey,” by manipulating the energy of the crowd and allowing the set to peak and flow. And this is where Mixology 6 takes on water and flounders. Rather than peaking and flowing, Bexta’s mix flatlines on the same energy level all the way through.

At the end of the day, I found Mixology 6 to be an average compilation. While her choices of individual tracks are often impressive, on the whole it seems like she’s not really trying. If you’re into hard trance and hardstyle then you can surely do a little better. Listening to it made me wish that Bexta would focus more on her production work and finally bring out her long overdue and eagerly awaited debut album. Anything but subject us to Mixology Vol. 7.
