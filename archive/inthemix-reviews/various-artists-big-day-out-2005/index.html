Various Artists - Big Day Out 2005

Angus Paterson · 18 January 2005 · inthemix

(EMI)

The Australian summer is an exciting time to be a music lover. With every live act and DJ you could possibly imagine lining up to fly over and enjoy the summer in the sun (and get paid exorbitant amounts of money to do so), local festival goers have a smorgasbord of events to choose from. The appeal of it all isn’t hard to understand: it gives the punters the chance to feel the good vibes, drink copious amounts of beer and celebrate the lifting of social restrictions by getting severely munted on hallucinogenic drugs. And for the last 12 years, the Big Day Out has been indisputably considered the cream of what is a rather delicious crop – for rock, dance and hip-hop lovers alike. For a taste of what we’ll all be boogying down to on a hot day in late January, the Big Day Out team have released the official souvenir compilation for the 2005 event as a companion for the incoming festival season.

The reason the Big Day Out has remained such a consistently shit-hot festival for so many years boils down to the ability of promoters Ken West and Vivian Lees to handpick artists that are either smokin’ hot already or on the cusp of breaking through at the time of the line-up announcement – only to be the bees knees by the time summer rolls around. Whatever their secret is, few would dispute they’ve managed to stay on top of what’s hot from year to year and make their festival a perfect barometer of music and cultural trends. If 2004’s festival was characterised by a re-emergence of balls-out 70’s rock, this year’s seems to be driven by a new diversity of rock, dance and hip-hop that has little room for musical limitations. With anticipation for the festival rising, the Big Day Out 2005 compilation features a list of acts that at the moment are, for want of a better term, the shit. With the Beastie Boys headlining the festival and the Chemical Brothers closing the main stage in Sydney, the line-up is more diverse than ever before and this is reflected in the companion CD.

With many of the tracks released several months ago or more there’s not a lot that you wouldn’t have already heard – what we’re given instead is a rock-solid collection of songs from an even more solid collection of musical acts. Kicking the compilation off with an unreleased track from New York hip-hop institution the Beastie Boys, it quickly moves through the power rock of The Donnas with Fall Behind Me, the quirky urban poetry of The Streets with Fit and You Know It to the earnest Aussie rock of Powderfinger’s Stumblin’. Much-loved bogan rock act Grinspoon continue their tradition of nonsensical lyrics with Hard Act to Follow, Spiderbait rock out with their unbeatable version of Black Betty while the Hives give the 70’s rock rebirth a tasty electronic twist with Two-Timing Touch and Broken Bones.

The Boiler Room has been an excellent source of pumping dance music for almost as long as the festival has been in existence, and hence electronic music lovers are not forgotten with this year’s CD. The glorious pop-infused breaks of the Freestylers is slotted neatly alongside New Zealand drum n’ bass act Concorde Dawn, while the Chemical Brothers consolidate their legacy with the new hip-hop slant of Get Yourself High. Straight-out hip-hop is also well represented on the CD, and the Beastie Boys are joined by New Zealand’s MC Scribe with his remix of Not Many.

To mention every single act would be a waste of time. All up there are 21 songs included on the CD, making sure that music lovers of all persuasions are catered for. All that needs to be known is that in every case, the acts are Class A quality. The only disappointment is the near total lack of any new material, meaning that Big Day Out 2005 falls well short of being a must-have release. But while this may be true, the compilation serves better as a release that will in a couple of years time make for a pretty good indication of what was hip, happening and relevant back in 2005. With a tracklisting as diverse as the festival line-up itself, it makes for a great companion for the summer festival season. Bring on the Big Day Out.
