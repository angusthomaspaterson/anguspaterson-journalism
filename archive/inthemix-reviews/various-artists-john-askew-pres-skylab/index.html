Various Artists - John Askew pres. Skylab

Angus Paterson · 6 April 2011 · inthemix

John Askew has been toiling away in the trance scene since way back in the mid 90s, guaranteeing his unquestionable veteran status. He’s known as much for his behind-the-scenes work in A&R for the Discover and Discover Dark labels as he is for the tough, muscular slabs of melodic energy favoured by the likes of Paul van Dyk.

With the amount of ‘pop trance’ infiltrating club sound systems growing exponentially over the years, Askew has more and more been looked to as one of the scene’s leading alternate voices alongside the likes of John ‘00’ Fleming. However, he’s never really cracked it big as a touring DJ, and his new Skylab compilation gives us every reason to believe his strengths lie elsewhere other than performing.

There’s a whole two discs of music here with more than 40 tracks, with an exciting range of fresh selections on offer. Askew opens with some lovely deep progressive trance, the type that used to be the bread and butter of Armin van Buuren’s prior to him being drawn like a moth towards the lights of stadium trance, with some awesome deeper selections like Audion’s Eleven Eleven, complemented by awesome vocal bangers from Mike Nichol as well as a divine remix from the untouchable Arty.

However, Askew is less than artful in his construction. You’ll be on the very edge of a deep, luscious melody, before you’re snapped out of your trance by messy mixing and the jarring transitions. There’s also a fair amount of faceless filler here, redeemed by some seriously epic trance in the second half – not the least of which is Sly One & Hurricane’s Everything to Me, with a deliriously euphoric vocal sample in the build-up, and a punchy closer from Tom Colontonio that seems destined to close things out nicely, before it’s faded out mid tune – a perfect example of how lazy he’s been in putting this release together.

The second disc gets off to a pumping and exhilarating start, firing off several tough and bubbling selections before grinding into Simon Patterson’s brutal anthem Whiplash – stirred up with generous doses of psychedelic and tech, it’s about as good as trance gets in 2011. This is supplemented by some excellent dark trancers from Sonic Element and Activa as it gradually moves into tougher, more psychedelic territory. Again though, it’s a journey that’s disappointingly let down by sloppy programming.

As much as you’d like to applaud Askew for his track selection, as someone who’s clearly got an A&R professional’s ear for quality tunes, there’s really next to no craft in drawing them together, and Skylab ultimately feels like a lazy collection of upfront tunes hodge-podged onto a double-CD. Nonetheless, Askew’s recent defection to Paul Oakenfold’s enduring Perfecto stable is definitely a tantalising prospect, because in spite of the CD’s flaws, Skylab still demonstrates that Askew knows his shit.

John Askew presents Skylab is out now through 405 Recordings.
