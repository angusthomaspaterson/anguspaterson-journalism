GT & Poxymusic - Clubbers Guide to... 2004

Angus Paterson · 6 June 2004 · inthemix

(Ministry of Sound)

When I last year reviewed the Ministry of Sound’s Clubbers Guide to… 2003, I claimed that while the local component was highly commendable, the contribution of the guest international superstar DJ left a lot to be desired. Well it looks like they’ve taken my comments on board, because for this year’s Clubbers Guide to… 2004 they’ve decided to ditch the old fogey Tall Paul and replaced him with a double shot of local content from Groove Terminator and Poxy Music.

It’s common knowledge that Groove Terminator has a great ear for what works well on the dancefloor, and that makes for a pretty apt description of his Clubbers Guide to… 2004 mix – a collection of floor rockin’ tunes. GT keeps the upfront beats rolling with a solid mix of house, breaks, and electro, and there are more than a few choice cuts on his CD. Poxy Music’s breaks edit of Ferry Corsten’s Rock Your Body Rock is just as certifiably rockin’ as the original tune, and Tomcraft’s pumping mix of Futureshock’s Late at Night stands out from the rest. Hook’s Step Right Up is a great example of how 80’s electro can be merged with more modern sensibilities to produce some pretty phat club sounds, and have no doubt, the new mix of Barcelona that Kid Kenobi, Ritual and Q45 have whacked together will be a freakin’ huge hit.

But while there’s no doubt that GT is a wicked DJ, there is something about his MOS mixes that never quite hit the spot and keep you coming back for more. With 21 tracks on the CD, there are so many tunes going in and out of the mix so quickly that it makes for a pretty shallow offering, and as the CD continues it feels more and more like a collection of big tunes slapped together. As any avid dance listener would know, its hard to maintain momentum if there are too many upfront tunes in a mix. Who knows, maybe it’s the restrictions of the MOS format; the label have obviously pressured GT to include Ben Benassi’s latest single, and AGAIN it features a synthesized woman’s voice laid over a fuzzy electro bassline. Bah.

Providing the duties for the second mix CD, the Poxy Music team (consisting of Ken Cloud and Pocket) has become quite the production duo to look out for lately. They’ve consistently delivered quality and have remixed some really big acts; just have a look at the tracklisting for their Clubbers Guide to… 2004 CD and try not to gawk, with them having worked on the likes of Kid Alex, The Freestylers, not to mention Ferry Corsten on GT’s CD. They begin with Pnau’s superstylin’ new breaks single We Love the Fresh Kills, which segues smoothly into the Stanton Warriors mix of Chicken Lips’ He Not In. Poxy Music then skim through a number of rock infused electro-house tunes for the majority of their CD.

Mixing is a more seamless affair that on GT’s CD – they can cram in 22 tracks and not make the mixing seem rushed, and it seems that they’ve been given more freedom in how they’ve compiled the tracklisting. But while there are more than a few of the emerging big breaks tunes littered throughout the mix (The Loose Cannon’s Superstar is fantastic and any contribution by The Plump DJs is always welcome) sometimes things becomes a little too quirky and self-indulgent for its own good. Meanwhile I sit back and wait for the electro fad to pass on into the realms of nostalgia yet again.

While the Aussie MOS CD’s have been consistently pretty damn good, Clubbers Guide to… 2004 is just a little bit harder to recommend. While one CD is fun but a little shallow, the other falls off the deep end into electro-quirkiness. While it’s a good release to grab if you simply want a collection of the biggest emerging tunes of the moment, dance music aficionados might want to steer clear. Unless you’re a sucker for electro that is.
