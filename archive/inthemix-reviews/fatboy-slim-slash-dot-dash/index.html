Fatboy Slim - Slash Dot Dash

Angus Paterson · 15 September 2004 · inthemix

(Skint/Sony)

Better than anyone else in the history of dance music, Fatboy Slim has mastered the art of taking mindless repetition and turning it into an insanely catchy pop song. Memorable smash hits like Rockafeller Skank and Star 69 speak for themselves, both examples of the Fatboy’s ability to grab a seemingly inane sample and create from it dance music that travels beyond commercial barriers. In anticipation of his upcoming album Palookaville, Fatboy Slim has done it again with his new single Slash Dot Dash. He’s taken a sample, repeated it ad infinitum over the course of the song – and in the process created a damn catchy tune.

While some may question Fatboy Slim’s ability to remain relevant so long after the groundbreaking success of You’ve Come a Long Way Baby, I urge you to dispel all of your reservations because Slash Dot Dash is one great big rockin’ and rollin slice of fun. Rockabilly dance music at its best, it shows Fatboy Slim’s growing interest in moving back towards live instrumentation. All the drums, guitars and bass on Slash Dot Dash are real – sampled in, sped up and shuffled around no less. The sample in question is of a loud American voice screaming relentlessly – yet as per usual, it somehow manages to bury its way into your head in an almost irresistible fashion.

Full of rockabilly guitars, old-school ‘rock n’ roll’ drum beats and a the odd bit of record scratching, Slash Dot Dash bops along at a blistering pace – and then within two and a half minutes it’s all over. While on the surface Norman Cook’s music may seem pretty mindless, underneath lies a very strong grasp of melody structure and musicianship, which belies the fact that he’s actually a classically trained musician. So as shallow as Slash Dot Dash may seem, as always Norman Cook is being deceptively clever.

Though more than a little bit of a throwaway tune, Slash Dot Dash proves that Fatboy Slim has still got it. But as the single I sampled was totally devoid of any remixes, b-sides or other album tracks, who knows what else Fatboy Slim has got in store for us? The indications are that Slash Dot Dash is not exactly representative of what awaits us on Palookaville, with the marketing types describing it as “the biggest and dumbest track off the whole album”. So I guess we’ll just have to wait and see.
