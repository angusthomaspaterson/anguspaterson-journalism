Various Artists - Y4K: Mixed by Uberzone

Angus Paterson · 4 July 2004 · inthemix

(Distinctive Breaks/DMC)

So what of nu-school breaks? To some they serve as an insult to the groovin’, hip-hop based groundwork from which breakbeat emerged. To others they provide an opportunity to hear some fresh sounds and cutting-edge production techniques, coupled with some unrestrained block rockin’ beats. But whether you dig nu-school or not, the Y4K series has always been an excellent avenue to showcase the style. Featuring first-class talent like Tayo, Hyper and Ils, DJ-producer Überzone has now taken up the reigns to deliver the next instalment. Überzone ranks up there in the upper-echelon of nu-school producers, and his Y4K contribution allows him to preach his techy and progressive style of breakbeat.

Grabbing your attention immediately from the word go, the opening song Soul Makossa is one of Überzone’s own production works, and makes for a superbly funky way to start the album. Fast moving and funky, squeezed all within the slickly produced song are big beats, a saxophone solo and the timeless vocal stylings of the one and only Africa Bambaata. Soul Makossa also demonstrates from the outset that Überzone is a production talent who ranks up there with all of the General Midi’s and B.L.I.M.’s of this world. The focus on upfront energy continues over the next several tracks, with the rolling beats and basslines complimenting the accomplished production. Überzone’s collaboration with Rennie Pilgrim on Fuergo is another example of dirty nu-school sounds that remain friendly to the clubs, while things get deep and sleazy with D. Ramirez’s Slave.

A real nice mixture of nu-school sounds can be found on Überzone’s Y4K compilation, and it cleverly shifts up and down the spectrum from the pumping cuts that start off the mix to the more crunchy, nastier stuff that emerges later on. Christian J & Meat Katie’s huge nu-school hit Turn It Up makes a welcome appearance, while General Midi’s Bass Rockers manages to mash up nu-school with some superbly funky 80’s electro, in a combination that is also destined to become a huge smash. What particularly impresses are the brief production titbits that Überzone occasionally squeezes in between tracks. While inclusions like the sexy percussiveness of Drunken Monkey don’t really count as individual songs, due to them being flung in and out of the mix so quickly, they succeed in adding to the tone and giving the mix that much more of an organic feel.

But it is Überzone’s decision to delve into some heavier and more esoteric sounds towards the mix’s end that holds back his Y4K CD from being something really commendable. Tracks like Paul Reset’s Wake Up!!! definitely gravitates towards the weirder side of breaks, and cause him to sacrifice the momentum that he had built up earlier on in the mix. There are some fine tracks among the eclectic offerings: Andy Page’s Mr Rush is a headfuck of a progressive tune, while Ferocious Mullet’s Cakewhole has a lovely organic feel to it. But after the rolling energy of the first half a dozen songs, you can’t help but feel that Überzone begins to wander aimlessly as soon as the weirder tracks begin to roll in.

With the current overabundance of breaks mixes flooding the market, Überzone may have trouble making an impact with his Y4K mix. With the compilation market as crowded as it is you have to deliver something of real quality to get noticed, and it’s highly possible that the latest Y4K could fall by the wayside. While there are sections that really get the blood pumping, overall it’s lacking just a little and fails to follow through on the promise the first half of the mix offers. But while it may be far from groundbreaking (or even particularly that memorable), Überzone’s contribution to the Y4K series certainly remains a solid effort. And for nu-school fans, it’s another quality collection of fresh tunes to add to their collection. Thus, to them it comes recommended.
