Paul Van Dyk - Global

Angus Paterson · 13 July 2003 · inthemix

(Vandit Records)

The # 1 name in trance over the last decade, Paul Van Dyk is one of dance music’s most enduring and prolific individuals when it comes to creative production work. For me, Paul Van Dyk has always personified everything that is good about trance – the power, the emotion, the euphoria. Whenever trance has strayed towards the cheesy generic side (as, sadly, it often does), Paul Van Dyk’s DJing and production work have allowed to me keep the faith. Now comes Global, a reflection on the history of his production work, which showcases his influence on the evolution of the sound.

Having been lucky enough to see Van Dyk when he toured Australia to promote Global, I witnessed a DJ at the top of his game. Looking down from the mezzanine level of Gas, PVD was sending shockwaves of energy through the crowd. Flawlessly building the set up and then pulling it back down, he took the crowd along with him every step of the way for three hours, displaying true passion for his craft. Global however puts the focus on PVD as a producer rather than a DJ; he has an absolutely huge back catalogue of albums, EP’s, singles and remixes. Global offers an exclusive look at this material, selecting the most memorable of his original compositions.

While a lot of the tracks on Global are taken from different periods of PVD’s career, they are all seamlessly mixed, so the effect is more like a journey. The CD begins with the memorable anthem We Are Alive, a euphoric selection that went down a treat during PVD’s set at Gas. This is followed by two selections from the 1996 album Seven Ways, including the title track and Forbidden Fruit. They both still sound great and have stood the test of time, surprising when you consider how quickly a lot of dance music seems to date. Forbidden Fruit is a particularly lush tune, featuring sweet subdued synth-lines that build into a break-filled chorus, finally peaking with a four-on-four beat.

A few selections from the 2000 masterpiece Out There and Back follow. Another Way still sounds as lush as it did back in 1999, featuring a heavenly intro that gets me each and every time, and Tell Me Why (The Riddle) soars with ethereal vocals from St Etienne. Both tracks are radio edits rather than the originals however, and funnily enough sound a tad more superficial when taken out of the context of the remarkable flow of the original album.

All of the older and more obscure songs hold up particularly well, with pretty much every track standing on its own as a unique piece of musical art, rather than one specifically designed for the clubs. Step Right On throttles up the tempo with an acidy bass line and uplifting chorus, sounding both harsh and euphoric at the same time, while Beautiful Place builds up and then bottoms out into a beautiful piano solo. Words, another cut from Seven Ways, has a vintage trance sound, while A Magical Moment is truly old-school.

One of the most notable mentions of course is a song released at a time when trance was exploding in the clubs around the world, For an Angel. Brilliantly constructed with real powerful emotion, For an Angel provided an anthem for a generation of clubbers and is a track that will last forever.

While both PVD fans and those interested in having a look at the history of his music will snap it up, with Global being essentially a greatest hits compilation it also suffers from the same weaknesses of other such similar releases. But while it may not enjoy the same cohesive and beautiful flow of an album like Out There and Back or Seven Ways, it remains nonetheless an essential purchase. Paul Van Dyk’s production work is never anything less than special – it contains energy, but is richly textured and multi-layered, never lacking in sincere emotion. While idealistically new material would have been nice, I suppose we’ll just have to wait until the release of his new studio album at the end of the year. A must-have for those who appreciate quality trance.
